# Stratigraphy of the Average Game

*By-decade league averages as geologic layers — an archaeology of pace, fouling, and the three-point ash*

If you treat a basketball season as a depositional event, the decade average is a lamina: thin, composite, dishonest about any single night, honest about a climate. The landed decade pack for the NBA’s average game (per-team per-game means, regular season, Basketball-Reference lineage) runs from a thin **1970s** slice through a still-incomplete **2020s**. Read top to bottom like a core, and the story is not “scoring went up.” The story is *what the sediment is made of*.

## The core log

Per-team per-game decade means (landed derived table):

| Layer | PTS | FGA | FG3A | FTA | OREB | TOV | PF | AST |
|-------|-----|-----|------|-----|------|-----|-----|-----|
| 1970s | 108.8 | 86.8 | 2.8 | 29.0 | 15.8 | 17.9 | 24.3 | 23.7 |
| 1980s | 109.1 | 85.9 | 2.5 | 29.2 | 15.0 | 18.0 | 24.6 | 24.0 |
| 1990s | 104.7 | 86.3 | 11.2 | 27.6 | 13.8 | 16.6 | 23.7 | 23.1 |
| 2000s | 99.8 | 86.1 | 16.1 | 27.9 | 13.0 | 15.0 | 22.3 | 22.2 |
| 2010s | 100.9 | 86.4 | 24.5 | 25.2 | 12.0 | 14.1 | 20.7 | 22.7 |
| 2020s | 108.8 | 88.8 | 35.0 | 21.9 | 10.9 | 13.7 | 19.2 | 25.6 |

Field-goal percentage in the same core wobbles in a narrow band—**0.481** (1970s), **0.486** (1980s), down to **0.450** (2000s), back to **0.468** (2020s)—while the *composition* of attempts rearranges the court. That is stratigraphy’s favorite joke: the bulk density looks stable; the mineralogy does not.

## Marker beds

**The three-point ash.** FG3A per team per game moves from **2.8** in the **1970s** lamina (effectively the **1979-80** introduction season in this aggregation) to **2.5** in the **1980s**, then **11.2**, **16.1**, **24.5**, and finally **35.0** in the **2020s**—a rise of **32.2** attempts per team per game across the core. Three-point percentage settles into the mid-**0.35**s from the **1990s** onward (**0.341** → **0.356** → **0.356** → **0.364**). The ash layer thickens; the ash itself gets slightly more efficient. Anyone who remembers when a three felt like a special effect is remembering a thinner bed.

**The free-throw unconformity.** FTA falls from **29.0** / **29.2** in the **1970s–80s** to **21.9** in the **2020s**—**7.1** fewer free-throw attempts per team per game—while FT% climbs from the mid-**0.75**s toward **0.783**. Fouling weather cools: personal fouls drop from **24.3** / **24.6** to **19.2**. The stripe at the line is still there; the river that fed it has narrowed.

**Offensive-rebound erosion.** OREB declines almost monotonically: **15.8 → 15.0 → 13.8 → 13.0 → 12.0 → 10.9**. Nearly **five** fewer offensive boards per team per game between the oldest and newest laminae. Defensive rebounds rise (**29.5 → 33.4** across the same span in the table), so total rebounds do not collapse—they *reallocate*. Misses that once became second chances become outlet passes into a spaced floor.

**Turnover thinning.** TOV eases from **17.9** / **18.0** to **13.7**—about **four** fewer giveaways per team per game. Care is not a vibe; it is a countable residue.

**Assist rebound (literal).** Assists sag through the defensive-era **2000s** (**22.2**) and then jump to **25.6** in the **2020s**—the highest layer in the pack—while points return to **108.8**, matching the **1970s** point total by a different chemistry: more threes, fewer free throws, fewer fouls, fewer turnovers, more ball movement on the box score.

## Landmark seasons as index fossils

Decade means smooth. Landmark seasons (separate light-pin sample, same BRef Per Game table) put named fossils in the wall:

| Season | Pin | PPG | Pace | FG% | 3PA rate (3PA/FGA) |
|--------|-----|-----|------|-----|---------------------|
| 1959-60 | 1950s end | 115.3 | — | .410 | — (pre-3PT) |
| 1985-86 | 1980s | 110.2 | 102.1 | .487 | 0.037 |
| 1996-97 | 1990s | 96.9 | 90.1 | .455 | 0.212 |
| 2018-19 | 2010s | 111.2 | 100.0 | .461 | 0.359 |
| 2023-24 | recent landmark | 114.2 | 98.5 | .474 | 0.395 |

Pace is blank before BRef’s published series (**pre-1973-74**); three-point fields are blank before the line. The **1996-97** fossil is the cold snap everyone feels in their bones: **96.9** PPG, **90.1** pace, a 3PA rate already past **0.20**. The **2023-24** fossil is not a return to **1959-60** so much as a different warm interval: scoring high again (**114.2**), pace near **100**, almost **two-fifths** of field-goal attempts from three (**0.395**). Archaeology’s rule applies: similar elevation, different assemblage.

## How to misread a core (and how not to)

Do not say the **2000s** “couldn’t score.” Say the average game deposited **99.8** points per team with **16.1** threes and still-high free-throw traffic. Do not say the **2020s** “invented offense.” Say the lamina combines **108.8** points with **35.0** threes, **21.9** free throws, and the pack’s highest assist rate. Do not invent pace for the **1959-60** pin; the cell is blank on purpose.

Caveats baked into the method notes travel with the metaphor: the **1970s** row in the decade CSV is a thin bed (three-point-era start), the **2020s** row is incomplete (four seasons in the compile), and these are team-level league averages, not play-by-play reconstructions. Stratigraphy is allowed to be approximate. It is not allowed to forge isotopes.

The average game, dug carefully, is a quiet artifact. It does not cheer. It stacks: ash of threes, silt of turnovers, a pale band where fouling thinned, a dark band where the mid-**90s** and **2000s** compressed scoring. Hold the core to the light and you can see why two eras with the same point total—**108.8** then, **108.8** now—are not the same rock.
