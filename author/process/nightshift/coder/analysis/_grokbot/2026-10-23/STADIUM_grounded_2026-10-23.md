## Post-2010 NBA Arena Financing: Public Contribution Patterns

### Dataset Overview

**Coverage:** 12 arenas, mostly opened 2012 onward; includes 2 NFL comparators (Allegiant, SoFi) for context.

**Core measure:** Public $ / Total construction cost = public subsidy %.

**Median public share (post-2010 NBA builds, ex-private):** ~43% (Golden 1, Little Caesars, Fiserv average).

**Range:** 0% (Warriors, Clippers self-financed) to ~47.7% (Fiserv, Milwaukee most subsidized).

### Financing Instruments in Use

1. **Tax-backed revenue bonds** (Golden 1, Little Caesars, Fiserv): City/county captures future incremental property-tax revenue or lease payments to repay debt. Transfers public risk to team operations.
2. **Dedicated revenue sources** (Golden 1 parking, Fiserv hotel tax): Fee on specific user behavior; less transparent to general public.
3. **TIF (Tax Increment Financing)** districts (Little Caesars, Fiserv): Diverts future property-tax growth in a zone to repay arena debt; impacts schools and other taxing bodies.
4. **Tax-exempt PILOT bonds** (Barclays, historical Staples): Payment in Lieu of Taxes structures; arena pays below full property tax in exchange for guaranteed payments.
5. **Land contribution** (Barclays, Intuit, Little Caesars): City/public authority contributes owned land; shifts acquisition cost to public.

### Data Gaps for STADIUM Chapter

1. **Construction detail by instrument:** Which subsidy instrument paid for which part? (e.g., City bonds for structure, state for parking, private for interior tech). Only Golden 1 disaggregates slightly ($272.9M bonds vs. parking revenue).
2. **SoFi Stadium (Rams/Chargers):** Reported as "privately financed" but actual public contributions not verified; construction cost range ($1.7–2.5B) unclear.
3. **Land value contribution:** For arenas built on public/formerly-public land (Barclays, Little Caesars, Intuit, Golden 1), no public assessment of land discount or opportunity cost.
4. **Long-term debt service:** Total interest paid over bond maturity. Data has principal only.
5. **Renovation/modernization $ post-opening:** Crypto.com Arena renovations (2022+) not costed; newer arenas' maintenance capital not addressed.
6. **Forgone revenue (tax exemptions):** Barclays' ongoing PILOT discount (~$99M annual property tax vs. ~$39M PILOT payment, per NYC IBO 2023) suggests public subsidy continues annually; not quantified for other arenas.

### Public Subsidy vs. "Community Benefits"

- **Direct capital subsidy:** Measured here (tax %, bonds, land).
- **Community benefits packages:** Intuit ($100M), others likely exist but not consistently reported. Often side agreements (parking discounts, youth facility access, naming rights for public areas); hard to monetize.
- **Forgone revenue:** Property-tax exemptions, sales-tax exemptions on construction, mortgage-recording tax breaks. Included implicitly in Barclays analysis; not quantified elsewhere.

**Observation:** Public share figures (%) may understate total public cost when ongoing tax exemptions are included.

### Comparative Context (NFL, Non-NBA)

- **Allegiant Stadium (Raiders, Las Vegas):** $1.97B construction, $750M public (38.1%), financed via hotel room tax (Stadium Authority cap $750M). Uses dedicated user fee rather than property tax TIF.
- **SoFi Stadium (Rams/Chargers):** Reported privately financed; public contribution unclear; not verified here.
