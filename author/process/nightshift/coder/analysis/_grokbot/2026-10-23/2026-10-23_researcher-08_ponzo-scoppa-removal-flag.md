**The request misdescribes the paper.** It is **not** a penalty-kick (shooter-under-pressure) study. It's about crowd support and the **home advantage** in Italian Serie A soccer:
- It covers 22 seasons, 1991-92 to 2012-13, and uses same-stadium derbies (Rome, Milan, Turin, Genoa, Verona) to separate crowd effects from travel and familiarity effects.
- Its abstract finds "a sizable crowd support's effect on the home advantage". It adds that crowd support "tends to bias referee's decisions (in terms of penalties, red cards, and yellow cards) in favor of the home team."
- Sources: the abstract via OpenAlex, and the full text of IZA DP 8105 attached in Zotero.

"Penalties" in the paper means penalties *awarded by referees*, not kicks taken under pressure. The classic penalty-kick choking paper is Dohmen 2008 (Bundesliga). I found no Dohmen 2008 item in the library and didn't add one.

## Recommendation (suggestion)
- **Remove it from the GAME TIME free-throw section.** It isn't evidence about executing a closed skill under pressure, so it shouldn't stand there even as a "non-basketball analogue". The basketball free-throw literature is rich enough that no soccer stand-in is needed.
- **Consider moving the citation to STADIUM / home-court advantage**, where it fits well. It's a clean natural experiment on crowd → referee bias, which is exactly what open request researcher-06 asks for ("recent causal or natural-experiment designs on ref bias mechanism"). There it should be labelled as a soccer study: "In Italian soccer, where city rivals share a stadium, …".
