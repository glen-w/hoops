## Core argument
Mental fatigue (MF) impairs sport-specific performance and decision-making, so it plausibly raises injury risk. This study gives a first prospective, field-based test in basketball.

## Methods
- Prospective observational study of **14 male semi-professional players** from one team in a Tier-3 interregional league over **21 weeks**.
- MF was measured on a visual analogue scale twice a week (MD-1 and MD+1). **482 of 588 questionnaires** were completed.
- Time-loss injuries were recorded by medical staff.
- Generalised logistic mixed models related MF to injury in the following windows, with Holm–Bonferroni correction.

## Key findings
- There were **11 time-loss injuries in 6 players**, 5 of them recurrent, an incidence of **7.40 per 1000 h**.
- Mean time lost was 12 ± 19 days. 91% of injuries happened in training and 73% were atraumatic.
- Mean MF was 44.1 ± 18.9 (on a 0–100 scale).
- **No significant association between MF and injury occurrence** in any window. Odds ratios were about 1.00–1.28.
- The authors frame the result as preliminary, given low statistical power. They call for larger and multi-team samples.

## Critical read
- This is a **null result from a very small sample** (11 events). It neither supports nor rules out an MF–injury link.
- One team and one season. Self-reported VAS measures. The indexed text doesn't name the country; the authors are based in Italy and Belgium.

## Fit with the chapter
