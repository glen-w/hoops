## Bottom line

- **New builds 2000–2024 with a usable split** (9 arenas, ~$6.2B nominal): the public paid **~$1.5B, about 25%**. The share is falling over time: Sun Belt 2001–04 builds (San Antonio, Houston, Memphis) ran **77–100% public**; 2016–18 Rust Belt/Sacramento builds ran **~38–48%**; Chase Center (2019) and Intuit Dome (2024) were **0%**. Those two billion-dollar private builds drag the average down.
- **Renovations are where the public money now goes.** 14 renovations with a split total ~$3.8B, of which **~45% public**. Charlotte (100%), Paycom/OKC (100%), Smoothie King (100%), Gainbridge (82%), AT&T Center (84%), State Farm (74%) and Capital One (64%) sit at the top; MSG ($1B), TD Garden and Wells Fargo Center were fully private.
- **The next wave (planned) is heavily public again.** OKC (≥$900M, ~94% public), Portland Moda (~$600M, ~95%), San Antonio (~$1.3B, ~62%) and Salt Lake City (public cap $525M for the arena alone).
- All amounts are **nominal USD, not inflation-adjusted**. "Public" means capital contributions (bonds, taxes, cash). It excludes land, tax abatements, operating subsidies and foregone property tax unless stated in the note.

## Method / caveats

- One row per project. Year = opening or renovation completion (planned rows use the target year or blank).
- `public_share_pct` is computed as public/cost where the source doesn't give it (my calc).
- Confidence: HIGH = government document or two consistent sources; MEDIUM = one good press source; LOW = search summary or conflicting figures.
- EDGAR full-text search adds nothing for most arenas because teams are private. The SEC data that exists is in coder-03 (MSG, Sphere, Live Nation, Braves).
- Zotero: no new items for this file. News and government pages were not added as journal articles, per the rules. Existing keys are cited where they exist (UKFPT79S, 5CDIBM4W, 7W5QESGJ, ECKR38KZ, 8WUDNV4X, ASQSSF2Q, plus the AAC keys).
