## Bottom line (suggestions, not orders)
- **Ballmer now controls two of LA's three big indoor arenas.** He owns the Clippers (bought in 2014) and Intuit Dome (opened Aug 2024). In 2020 his company CAPSS LLC bought the Kia Forum from MSG for $400M, which ended MSG's lawsuits against the arena. AEG keeps Crypto.com Arena (Lakers, Kings, Sparks).
- **The land price is now found.** On 8 Sep 2020 Inglewood gave final approval to sell the Clippers 13 parcels for **$66.25M**. About 22 of the 28 acres were city-owned. The arena budget was $1.8B, with a **$100M community benefits package** and local-hire targets. This fills the 23 Oct "not found".
- **Market effect:** the Clippers' move freed 40+ dates at Crypto.com Arena, which hosted 238 events in its first year without them (Pollstar). The Forum stays a concert venue under the same owner as Intuit Dome.
- **Displacement:** the reporting is strong but the causal evidence is weak. Capital B reports Inglewood lost more than 11,000 Black residents, and links this to the arena and Olympics boom. The developer-commissioned EIR displacement study (2019) found no link. No peer-reviewed study isolates the arena's effect. Lauermann's study of 472 stadium neighbourhoods is descriptive, not causal.
- **CBAs** are the main tool, and the LA Live CBA (2001) is the best-studied case. It delivered on some promises, but housing credits were stretched: AEG got credit for 120 units when Ritz condos triggered 45 (Saito & Truong 2015).

## What the book can safely say
Steve Ballmer built Intuit Dome with private money on land Inglewood sold him for $66.25 million. Along the way he bought the rival Forum for $400 million, which ended the lawsuits against the project. The team promised a $100 million community benefits package. Inglewood's Black population has fallen sharply as rents rose around the new stadiums, but no study has yet separated the arena's effect from SoFi, the Olympics and LA's wider housing crisis.
