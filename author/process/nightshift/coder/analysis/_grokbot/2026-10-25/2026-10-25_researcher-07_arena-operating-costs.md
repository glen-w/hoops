# Arena upkeep: renovation cycles, who pays, license fees and green certification

**Request:** 2026-10-24-pm-researcher-07 · **Status:** partly done (public deal terms found; per-arena operating budgets, concessions labour costs and utility bills are not public) · **Prepared by:** Grok Bot nightshift, 25 Oct 2026 (Europe/Paris) · Zotero keys in [brackets]

## Bottom line (suggestions, not orders)
- **The big recurring cost is the renovation, and it is mostly public.** NBA arenas built in the 1990s–2000s are being rebuilt at 17–30 years old, at $215M–$800M each, and cities usually pay most of it in return for longer leases. Three public examples: Charlotte $215M (2022), Phoenix $230M with the city paying up to $150M (2019), Washington DC $515M public out of ~$800M (2024).
- **Leases build the cycle in.** Charlotte's lease obliges the city to keep the arena "among the most modern in the league": if more than half of NBA arenas have an amenity, the Hornets can ask for it (WFAE). Phoenix's earlier deal had an "obsolescence clause" that could have forced the city to pay whatever an arbitrator decided (Cronkite News). Those clauses are why renovations come round on schedule.
- **Day-to-day running costs are usually the team's.** Phoenix: the Suns pay operations and maintenance; the city pays major renovations, and both pay into a renewal fund ($2M city + $1M team per year). Per-arena operating budgets are not published, so I found **no** reliable dollar figure for annual arena running costs. Mark as unknown.
- **Where a team rents from a sister company, the rent is visible.** MSG Sports' 10-K shows the Knicks paid ~$22.5M (Rangers ~$16.7M) arena license fees in the first full year (FY2021), rising 3% a year to 2055.
- **Why owners keep renovating:** the "novelty effect" fades. New NBA arenas sell ~294,000 extra tickets over their first nine seasons (Coates & Humphreys 2005). In MLB, newer ballparks' attendance bump lasts 6–10 seasons (Clapp & Hakes 2005).
- **Green certification is now a selling point, but it's self-reported or vendor-reported.** Golden 1 Center (2016) was the first LEED Platinum indoor sports venue. Climate Pledge Arena (2023) was the first ILFI Zero Carbon certified arena, partly by buying >100,000 MWh of renewable energy certificates. Intuit Dome is reported LEED Platinum by its architect. No independent studies of running-cost savings were found.

## What the book can safely say
NBA arenas are not built once. Around 15–25 years in, leases and "state-of-the-art" clauses trigger rebuilds costing hundreds of millions, and cities usually pay most of it to keep the team: $215M in Charlotte, up to $150M in Phoenix, $515M in Washington. Day-to-day costs are typically the team's, but nobody publishes them. Green labels like LEED Platinum and Zero Carbon are now part of the pitch for new buildings. They are real certifications, but the savings claims come from the teams themselves.
