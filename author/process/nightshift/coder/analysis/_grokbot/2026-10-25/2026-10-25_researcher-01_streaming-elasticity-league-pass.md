# researcher-01: Amazon Prime Video deal, League Pass demand, blackout economics, regional pricing
Night: 2026-10-24-pm, written 2026-10-25. All fetch dates are 2026-10-25 unless stated otherwise. Only public sources were used; SBJ and Sportico were not accessed behind their paywalls.

## Bottom line
**No public source gives League Pass subscriber counts, churn, or price-response data. That means a League Pass price elasticity cannot be estimated from public information.** What the public record does show:
- The list price **did not change** when distribution moved to Prime Video in 2025-26. The increase came a year earlier, in 2024-25.
- The NBA reported League Pass subscriptions **up 10%** in the opening month of 2025-26, but gave no base number.
- Over the same period, the vMVPD channel was cut: YouTube TV and Fubo lost League Pass.

So the one natural experiment (the move to Prime) is mixed up with a channel change and a strong ratings year. Any book claim should read "demand rose after the switch (league-reported, +10%)" and not "League Pass is price-inelastic".

## 1. What Amazon bought (primary source)
Amazon MGM Studios press release, 24 Jul 2024 — https://press.amazonmgmstudios.com/us/en/press-release/amazon-prime-video-and-the-nba-announce-landmark-1 (Zotero FNACSM6C):
- **Term:** 11 years from 2025-26.
- **Games:** 66 exclusive regular-season games, all NBA Cup knockout games, the entire Play-In, about one-third of rounds 1–2, and the Conference Finals in 6 of 11 years.
- **League Pass:** "Prime Video Channels will also be the strategic partner and third-party global channels store destination for NBA League Pass … in the U.S. and internationally."
- **Expanded package** in **Mexico, Brazil, France, Italy, Spain, Germany, UK and Ireland:** at least 20 extra primetime regular-season games, a Conference Finals series every year, and the **Finals in 6 of 11 years**.
- **International game rights:** worldwide except **Greater China, Poland, Finland, Sweden, Norway, Denmark and the Netherlands**. Canada starts in 2026-27.
- **Extras:** half of Summer League, plus a G League package.
- **Price:** not in the release. The ~$1.8B a year figure is press-reported (see the 23 Oct CSV row; NBA umbrella release FT9EDPT8, reused).

## 2. League Pass distribution after the switch
- CableTV.com, 25 Nov 2025 — https://www.cabletv.com/news/nba-league-pass-amazon-prime-exclusive (UTK2MMJZ):
  - Prime is now the **only digital/vMVPD channel store** for League Pass. YouTube TV dropped it on 30 Sep 2025, and Fubo dropped it too.
  - Some cable MSOs (e.g., Optimum) and DirecTV still carry it, and direct purchase from the NBA still works.
  - It quotes NBA executive Jeff Kaiser: "If you want to subscribe to League Pass starting next year and watch on a digital platform, you'll need to subscribe through Prime Video."
- NBA help centre: League Pass can be bought through Prime Video Channels in **33 countries**. Source: https://support.watch.nba.com/hc/en-us/articles/34813181782679 (fetched 2026-10-25; not added to Zotero, help-centre page).

## 5. Blackout economics
- **Mechanics** (NBA League Pass Blackout Guide — https://support.watch.nba.com/hc/en-us/articles/115002481154-League-Pass-Blackout-Guide, 5ZG4AZ38):
  - **US:** local-team games and national (ESPN/ABC, NBC/Peacock, Prime) games are blacked out live. Local replays appear after 3 days; national replays at 6am ET the next day.
  - **Canada:** national games only.
  - **Elsewhere:** no blackouts.
  - Location comes from IP address or ZIP code. Audio stays available.
  - Fetching this page with curl returned a Cloudflare check. Its content was read earlier tonight with WebFetch.
- **What blackouts are for:** they protect two other revenue streams. One is the local RSN or team-owned in-market rights. The other is the exclusivity of the three national partners. In-market fans are pointed to **team-owned DTC products** (MavsTV+, ClipperVision, Suns+ on NBA help pages), and national blackouts still apply to those. The League Pass price therefore covers out-of-market viewing only. It is a **segmented price**, which is the textbook reason blackouts exist.
- **Academic anchors** (no causal NBA blackout study was found, as on 23 Oct):
  - Gropper & Anderson (2016), "Sellout, Blackout, or Get Out", *Journal of Sports Economics* 19(3):332-370, doi:10.1177/1527002516641165 (FRXFE9WH, other sports). NFL 2012 policy change: **no evidence** that teams adopting the policy had fewer blackouts or different attendance. Crossref also lists a second DOI for the same title (10.1177/1527002516661600, 19(4):522-561). It looks like a duplicate publication record; I filed the 19(3) version only.
  - Kunz (2020), "Streaming and Out-of-Market Services", in *The Political Economy of Sports Television*, Routledge, pp. 124-149, doi:10.4324/9780429341113-6 (IFHIFSZS). This is the political-economy account of out-of-market packages.
  - Already in Zotero, cited and not re-added: Mongeon & Winfree 2012 (7KDKCK67), Foster et al. 2014 (AW9D2F6S), Kunz 2017 (5JN7VUDR), Salaga et al. 2020 (KVJVMKV3).
- **Lead, not researched tonight:** the public court records and settlements in *Laumann v. NHL* / *Garber v. MLB* (2015–16) and the 2024 NFL *Sunday Ticket* jury verdict, which was later overturned. These are the main public evidence on out-of-market package pricing. Worth one evening if the chapter needs an "antitrust of blackouts" paragraph.
