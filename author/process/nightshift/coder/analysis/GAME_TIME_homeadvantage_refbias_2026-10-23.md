# GAME TIME Chapter: Home Advantage and Referee Bias (Grounded)

**Sources:** Grokbot 2026-10-23 (request researcher-06). Status: ready for manuscript.

## Core Finding: COVID Natural Experiment Shows Fans, Not Refs, Drive Home Advantage

### Home Advantage Collapsed Without Fans
- **With fans (2019 and prior):** ~+2.1 to +2.55 points/game for home team
- **Empty arenas (2020–21 COVID):** ~+0.39 to +0.44 points/game
- **Elasticity:** ~1.7 points per 1,000 fans (Ganz & Allsop 2023, published, not 1.4 working-paper figure)
- **Sample sizes:** 440 games without fans, 507 with fans, six prior seasons for baseline

### Referee Calls Show Little Crowd Bias in NBA
- **Last Two Minute Reports (most objective calls):** no crowd-caused home bias (Gong 2022)
- **Attendance variation in empty arenas:** small crowds kept advantage; officials not measurably influenced (Ehrlich & Potter 2023)
- **Foul margin:** no significant change in home foul advantage between fans/no-fans (Ganz & Allsop 2023, multiple studies)
- **Discretionary calls only:** pre-COVID studies find refs favor home teams on judgment calls (Price, Remer & Stone 2012), but this effect disappeared when tested post-COVID

### The Mechanism: Player Effort and Energy (Mainly Rebounding)
- **Mediation analysis:** home win rate 58.65% with crowds vs 50.60% without; rebounding (effort proxy) mediates most of the difference (Leota et al. 2022)
- **Implication:** fans affect player effort (hustle, focus), not how officials call the game

## Important Caveat: Soccer Is Different

- **European soccer empty-stadium studies:** empty stadiums clearly made referees fairer to away teams (Pettersson-Lidbom & Priks 2010 landmark; Bryson et al. 2021; Scoppa 2021)
- **Mechanism is opposite:** refs *were* biased by crowds; emptying stadiums reduced bias
- **EuroLeague basketball (mixed results):** home advantage fell from ~70% to ~51% with fans absent; free-throw attempts (discretionary) shifted (Paulauskas et al. 2022); Spain ACB showed foul shift but EuroLeague did not (Jiménez et al. 2025)
- **Frame:** keep basketball and soccer apart; they respond differently

## Pre-COVID Baseline: Refs Favored Home on Discretionary Calls

**Source:** Price, Remer & Stone (2012) *J Econ Mgmt Strategy* 21(1):271–300 [RKPR3BR2], DOI 10.1111/j.1530-9134.2011.00325.x

- Home teams and teams trailing in a game or series get more judgment-call turnovers
- Best pre-2020 evidence of referee favoritism in basketball
- Post-COVID findings don't reproduce this strongly (calls more even overall, not biased)

## What the Book Can Safely Say

When COVID emptied NBA arenas, home-court advantage almost vanished: from about two points a game to under half a point. The best estimate is that every 1,000 fans add roughly 1.7 points to the home team's margin (Ganz & Allsop 2023). But when researchers looked at the referees' calls, including the league's own Last Two Minute Reports, they found little sign that NBA officials had been tilting calls toward the home team because of the crowd. The fans seem to work mainly through the players—their effort, focus, and rebounding. Soccer tells a different story: there, empty stadiums clearly made referees fairer to away teams.

## Known Gaps

- **No verified NBA free-throw attempt study** (fans vs. empty). The "~1.3 FTA per game home advantage" claim is unsourced.
- **Crowd noise causality:** tested experimentally in soccer and in basketball for anxious refs (2PBTRNY6 in library), but not causally in NBA games
- **2020–21 travel effects:** McHill & Chinoy separated fan effects from travel/circadian in bubble, but interaction terms not fully decomposed
