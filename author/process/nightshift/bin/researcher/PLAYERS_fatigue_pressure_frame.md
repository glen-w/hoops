# Fatigue and Pressure: Which Skill Breaks?

One hypothesis in sports psychology is that fatigue causes choking—that tired athletes reinvest effort and attention into a skill, which backfires. Another is that fatigue simply removes the mental resources that explicit, rule-based skills rely on, so players whose shooting is consciously controlled suffer most.

The literature supports both, but differently. Exercise-induced (physical) fatigue does transiently hurt some executive measures, though the effects are often small and depend on intensity. More relevant to basketball: mental fatigue and ego depletion—the sense of having depleted self-control—*do* worsen free-throw performance under pressure. An experiment by Englert & Bertrams (2012) had basketball players perform a Stroop task (depleting attention) or a simple task (control). Those who were depleted then shot worse on free throws as their state anxiety rose. Non-depleted players showed no such relation.

The twist: in studies of skill learning, fatigue (both physical and mental) hurts *explicitly* learned skills—those taught via rules and conscious mechanics—while *implicitly* learned skills—those acquired through analogy and feel—hold up (Poolton et al. 2007; Masters et al. 2008; Banihosseini et al. 2025). This suggests an alternative frame: fatigue doesn't cause reinvestment; it removes the working-memory resources that explicit, consciously controlled shooting relies on.

Which frame applies to an NBA player depends on how they learned to shoot. A player whose free throw is grounded in rules ("elbow in," "follow-through," "rhythm") would suffer more under fatigue. A player who shoots by feel, or who has integrated the mechanics so deeply that they're automatic, might be less affected. The full chain—fatigue → measured cognitive decline → reinvestment failure → free-throw outcome—has not been tested in the same people. But the fatigue–skill-control link is strong enough to suggest that in high-workload games, players who rely on conscious control may be more vulnerable.
