**Competitive Balance and Revenue Disparities: The Soft Salary Cap's Structural Failure**

The NBA's soft salary cap (51% of projected basketball-related income) and luxury tax (61% threshold) were designed to enforce competitive balance. Yet empirical evidence reveals systematic failure. {Totty & Owens, 2011} found no statistical improvement in balance post-cap and emphasise that caps cannot address the root cause: revenue disparities amongst franchises. {Endo et al., 2003} documented that the Gini coefficient for wins actually increased in the salary-cap era, contradicting expectations of convergence. {Larsen et al., 2006} acknowledged that whilst caps theoretically promote balance, statistical models show negligible effects. {Pradhan & Leshchinskii, 2025} shift focus to whether luxury tax penalties justify competitive gains, revealing a crucial gap between mechanism and outcome. The underlying problem is architectural: market-driven revenue differences—stemming from market size, media deals, and ownership capital—persist independently of payroll constraints. Salary caps redistribute spending but cannot eliminate structural inequality.
