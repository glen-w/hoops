# Wellness Through Play: Pickup Basketball as Self-Care

Grounded in: Sonkeng & Chepyator-Thomson (2020), *Exploring the healing touch of pickup basketball as a self-care method for educators and helping professionals*

## The Gap in Research

For decades, basketball's role in mental and physical health remained largely unstudied. Sonkeng's ethnographic work fills this gap by asking a deceptively simple question: what makes pickup basketball therapeutic?

## Key Mechanisms of Healing

**Affirmation and Empowerment**
Participants in the study—educators, helping professionals, trauma survivors—described pickup as a space of validation. After emotionally draining work, the game offers both recognition and physical release.

**The Physicality of Touch**
Basketball's unique structural feature: close proximity without protective gear. This creates abundant opportunities for affirming contact—high fives, handshakes, hugs—that transcend the usual distance of professional life. Touch becomes a language of inclusion.

**Transcending Boundaries**
The study found healing crossing race, gender, culture, and class. Pickup basketball functions as what participants called a "universal language"—a space where social hierarchies pause.

**Accessibility and Abundance**
Unlike structured therapy or medical intervention, pickup requires a court, a ball, and willing players. It is everywhere and costs nothing. This accessibility makes it a genuine public health resource, not a luxury.

## Implications for Practice

The authors recommend:
- Creating skill-level-specific runs to reduce intimidation barriers
- Encouraging helping professionals to integrate local pickup into their own wellness
- Raising public awareness through social and professional networks

Pickup basketball is not metaphorically healing. It is literally self-care—embodied, affordable, and waiting.
