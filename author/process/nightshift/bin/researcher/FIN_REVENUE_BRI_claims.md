Basketball-Related Income (BRI) encompasses all NBA revenue—gate receipts, television contracts, merchandise, and ancillary income. The Collective Bargaining Agreement allocates approximately 50–51 per cent of BRI to players, with the remainder supporting team operations and ownership returns. This framework, substantially revised in the 2023 CBA, emerged from decades of labour negotiations and fundamentally shapes franchise economics (Union, 2023). Revenue sharing operates via two mechanisms: explicit transfers from high-revenue to low-revenue franchises, and the salary cap structure, which constrains spending to a percentage of league-wide BRI. Despite these levelling devices, market-size disparities persist—New York, Los Angeles, and Miami benefit disproportionately from larger local television contracts and gate revenues (League, 2023). The BRI distribution framework is essential for understanding franchise valuations, competitive balance, and long-term viability in smaller markets. Recent modifications have shifted negotiating leverage towards players, increasing their share and tightening salary cap calculations.
