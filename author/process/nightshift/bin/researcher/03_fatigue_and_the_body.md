# Chapter 03: Fatigue and the Body

## Kinematics, Movement Degradation, and Injury Risk

Fatigue is not a single thing. It is a cascade: depletion of muscle glycogen, accumulation of lactate and hydrogen ions, central nervous system fatigue, and psychological fatigue. Each has a different time course and affects performance differently.

Rusdiana and colleagues (2020) examined how fatigue alters the kinematics of the free throw—the spatial and temporal properties of movement. They tracked center of mass, elbow and wrist joint angles, angular velocities, and ball release variables in players who completed a fatigue protocol of sprints and jumps. The literature on this question is mixed: some studies find no significant kinematic change after fatigue, while others detect subtle shifts in joint angles and release mechanics.

This mixed evidence reflects the complexity of human motor control. Elite players, trained to shoot thousands of times, develop a movement pattern that is robust to some forms of fatigue. The core motor program persists. Yet at the margins—in the precision of release angle, in the timing of hip drive, in the consistency of follow-through—fatigue introduces noise.

More concerning is the injury risk. Sansone and colleagues conducted a mixed-methods study of mental fatigue across a basketball season, while broader research on fatigue and injury risk surveyed high-performance sport practitioners. Those practitioners, who live in the world of athlete health, perceive a clear link between fatigue and acute noncontact injuries. The proposed mechanisms include impaired motor control, poor biomechanics, and reduced cognitive function—exactly the factors that degrade the consistency of movement.

A player in the fourth quarter of a tight game, having played 35 minutes and with mental fatigue accumulated from decision-making and stress, is not moving the same way as in the first quarter. The risk is not just that the shot misses. It is that the foot plants wrong, the knee collapses, and the season ends.

The body does not separate the fatigue of the game from the fatigue of the mind. Both are real.
