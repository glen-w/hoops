# Beyond the Box Score: Measuring True Performance

Grounded in: Marmarinos et al. (2019), *Structural validity and reliability of new index for evaluation of high-level basketball players*

## The Problem with Incomplete Metrics

For years, basketball evaluation relied on simple metrics—points, rebounds, assists—or on the Performance Index Rating (PIR), which still fails to capture the full scope of what a player contributes. Marmarinos' research reveals a better way.

## The Total Performance Index (TPI)

The authors developed the TPI using three integrated components:

**Offensive Contribution**
A scoring-accuracy weighted system that credits both shot efficiency and point creation (scoring + assists − turnovers), adjusted for offensive rebounding and team pace.

**Defensive Contribution**
Measures both what a player gains (rebounds, steals, blocks) and what pressure they exert (forcing opponent inefficiency), scaled to team defensive pace.

**Possession Efficiency**
Accounts for the player's share of team defensive possessions and their defensive stops, preventing the metric from inflating stats in high-volume situations.

**Formula**: TPI = Offensive Part + Points Offered − Points Received

## Why It Matters: The Evidence

Analyzing 442 players across Euroleague 2016–18:

- **Validity**: TPI explained 64.6% of team winning percentage (R² = 0.646) vs. PIR's 32.7%
- **Practical significance**: One unit increase in TPI = 1.8% increase in team wins (vs. 1.3% for PIR)
- **Reliability**: TPI predicted 58.5% of next-season value for same-team players (r = 0.765), but crucially, 38.5% for those changing teams (r = 0.62)—suggesting the metric captures player skill, not team environment

PIR failed statistical significance when players changed teams, indicating it conflates individual performance with context.

## What This Means

TPI is not just more accurate—it is more *portable*. A player's true contribution is visible independent of team quality, making it a tool for fairer evaluation, trade analysis, and player development. The box score, once sufficient, now gives way to a metric that honors basketball's true complexity.
