# STADIUM: Arena Design, Construction, and Public Finance

NBA arena design has evolved through two distinct eras. The "Golden Age" (1920s) featured compact venues averaging 3.12 acres; the "Me" Arena facilities (1950–1989) introduced cable-supported and domed-roof structures eliminating internal support poles, expanding average footprint to 8.70 acres [S4]. The Oakland Coliseum Arena employed 96 steel cables supporting a 420-foot-diameter, 6.5-million-pound roof [S4]. By the late 1980s–1990s, design philosophy shifted toward entertainment orientation. Stern (cited in S5) noted that "every arena in the NBA between 1987 and 2000 would have been new or rebuilt...with suite boxes, club seats and increased revenues" [S5].

Construction costs remained substantial. Key Arena (Seattle) cost $7 million to build; a 1995 renovation cost $74.5 million [S3]. Charlotte Coliseum cost $52 million [S3]. Both were 100% publicly financed. Propheter (cited in S2) observes that by the 1980s, NBA municipalities negotiated "lucrative arena financing deals," with Santo (2007) estimating Portland's public subsidy at 47% of the $235 million total arena cost [S2]. Multi-purpose configurations—e.g. Key Arena seating 17,072 for basketball, 15,177 for hockey—maximised revenue streams [S3].
