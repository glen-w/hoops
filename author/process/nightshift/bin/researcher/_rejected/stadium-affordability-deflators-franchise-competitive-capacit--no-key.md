Arena construction costs escalated sharply from 2000 to 2026 as measured by the Engineering News-Record Construction Cost Index (20-city, 1913=100): from 6,221 to 10,784, a 73% nominal increase. This cost escalation coincided with a shift in arena financing: post-2010 projects shifted financial risk to private franchise operators through revenue bond structures (Detroit's 2018A senior TI refunding, $287.4M par; Orlando's 2017A senior TDT refunding, $159.9M outstanding). Franchise owners captured naming rights and corporate sponsorship revenue while public authorities bore construction overrun risk. This asymmetry disadvantaged smaller-market franchises unable to secure premium sponsorship deals, constraining their ability to build modern, high-capacity venues (18,000–20,000 seats). Arena capacity became a proxy for franchise competitive position, as seating affects gate revenue, luxury box pricing, and fan experience quality.
