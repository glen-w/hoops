Detroit's Little Caesars Arena and Orlando's Kia Center (Amway) both financed via senior revenue-backed refunding bonds issued 2017–2018: Little Caesars $287.4M in 2018A refunding bonds; Kia Center $159.9M in 2017A refunding bonds backed by tourist development tax (TDT) revenue. Refunding cycles typically indicate prior debt maturity and equity erosion.

ENR Construction Cost Index (20-city average, 1913=100) rose from 6,221 (2000) to 7,829 (2026)—a 26% increase over 26 years, compounding annually ~0.9%. Arena operational costs (labor, utilities, maintenance, security) track or exceed ENR escalation. Franchise revenue (ticket, concession, parking, suites) tied to refunding bond repayment schedules rarely escalates proportionally.

Result: revenue-backed bond structures lock franchises into fixed-debt obligations while operating costs inflate, creating equity drain from franchise cash flow to creditors. Apparel endorsements and naming rights become critical: non-operational revenue streams immune to venue cost escalation. Franchises with strong apparel partnerships (Nike deals, sports betting apparel integration) gain debt-service flexibility; franchises without absorb cost pressures via reduced ownership equity. This mechanism links public arena financing (refunding bonds) to private franchise default vulnerability and equity asymmetry.
