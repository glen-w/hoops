# NBA Arena Refunding Debt: Structural Analysis of Detroit and Orlando (2018–2024)

**Sources:** Little Caesars Arena (Detroit) bond documents; Amway Center (Orlando) bond disclosure; City of Detroit and City of Orlando official statements.

Both arenas employed senior refunding bond structures post-2010 to manage capital obligations. Little Caesars (2018A round) issued $287.4M in senior tax-increment refunding bonds, financed via Downtown Development Authority revenue. Amway Center (Orlando/Kia Center) issued $159.9M in senior tourism development tax refunding bonds (2017A), backed by the 6th Cent Contract Payments mechanism.

These structures distribute financial risk: public tax revenues (TIF, TDT) secure debt service, while franchises retain operational upside. Both arenas required ongoing tax-backed subsidy flows, illustrating how post-2010 public arena financing shifted toward debt restructuring rather than new-build capital campaigns. Refunding mechanisms allowed municipalities to refinance earlier municipal bonds while preserving franchise revenue claims.

ENR CCI deflators (2000–2026) show cumulative construction cost inflation exceeding general CPI, suggesting arena financing strategies increasingly relied on tax-backed borrowing to absorb real cost escalation. This trend reflects growing public-private imbalance in arena capital structures.
