# Nominal Debt, Refunding Cycles, and Equity Drain in Arena Bonds

Refunding cycles in revenue-backed arena bonds create persistent equity drain through nominal debt accumulation. Analysis of Little Caesars Arena (Detroit, 2018A bonds: $287.4M par) and Amway Center (Orlando, 2017A bonds: $159.9M par) reveals how public authorities refinance outstanding debt at par or above, extending maturities without reducing principal.

From 2000–2026, the ENR CCI (20-city Construction Cost Index) escalated from 6,221 (index) to 10,897, representing 75% cumulative inflation. This cost escalation directly increases O&M obligations backed by revenue streams (ticket sales, naming rights, concessions), compressing equity available to the public authority.

Refunding mechanics: senior debt is retired with new bonds at nominal par, often rolling basis points and extending maturity. The gap between inflation-adjusted costs and fixed revenue-bond coverage ratios narrows; equity drain accelerates. The franchise retains operating profits; the public authority absorbs cost escalation risk and refinancing risk. Asymmetric default exposure favors the franchise operator.

Apparel and naming rights provide short-term liquidity stabilization but insufficient to offset structural equity erosion across 20–30 year bond terms.

**Sources:** DDA Detroit 2018A prospectus; City of Orlando Bond Disclosure; ENR CCI (2000–2026).
