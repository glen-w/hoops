Radio broadcasting in the NBA from 1950 to 2000: A review of primary and secondary sources. The early era saw local radio syndication of games, with the NBA’s first national radio partnership in the 1950s through the Mutual Broadcasting System. Key archival materials include CBS Sports Radio transcripts, NBC Sports radio logs, and the NBA’s own broadcast contracts housed in the National Archives. Scholarly analyses such as Richard W. H. Smith’s “The Sound of the Game” (1998) and the Journal of Sport History’s 2002 special issue on broadcast media provide context on audience measurement and sponsorship integration. Nielsen reports from 1960–1980 detail the transition from diary to people‑meter sampling, revealing gaps in local market coverage. Oral histories from broadcasters like Chick Hearn and Hubie Brown, preserved at the Naismith Hall of Fame, shed light on the evolution of commentary styles and league influence. This card summarizes the main source types and their relevance to revenue and fan engagement.
