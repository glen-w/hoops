## Key Factors Affecting Shooting Efficiency in Basketball

The excerpts identify several interrelated factors that influence shooting efficiency:

### Shot Mechanics, Practice, and Physical Condition
Shooting is described as a specific skill that requires extensive practice and repetition until it becomes nearly automatic [S2]. Proper shot mechanics, good physical condition of the player, and emotional stability are highlighted as important determinants of shooting efficiency [S2]. Physical and psychological fatigue can cause "motor conversion" that disrupts proper shot mechanics [S2]. Similarly, fatigue and defensive pressure have been shown to significantly affect the kinematic parameters of the three-point shot, with players altering jump height and shooting technique to maintain consistency [S6].

### Defensive Pressure and Game Context
Defensive pressure is a recurring factor. Csataljay et al. note that the highest shooting efficiency is reached when a team creates situations near the basket without the active presence of defenders [S3]. Defensive philosophy can also shape shooting outcomes: coaches may force opponents into less effective mid-range jump shots rather than allowing layups or set three-pointers [S3]. Terner & Franks frame this more broadly, noting that shot efficiency varies across players, over space, and in defensive contexts [S4]. In women's basketball specifically, the success of three-point shooting depends on game context and the specific characteristics of the women's game [S1].

### Shot Selection and Volume
Terner & Franks identify two key drivers of scoring ability: the ability to selectively identify the highest-value scoring options (shot selection) and the ability to make a shot conditioned on an attempt (shot efficiency) [S4]. They also reference Dean Oliver's "skill curve," which reflects an inverse relationship between a player's shot volume and shot efficiency [S4].

### Psychological and Cognitive Dimensions
Psychological factors are emphasized as very important for player success [S2]. In the context of three-point shooting, coaches and players need to understand not only shooting technique but also the psychology and visual aspects that affect success [S6].

### Equipment and External Conditions
The size of the basketball can affect shooting accuracy; one study found that introducing a smaller (size 6) ball did not improve general shooting accuracy for female players, though it increased the number of three-point attempts [S7].
