# Brayton Chapter: Concussion Crisis and Labor Politics

## Research Anchor
**Source:** Brayton (2019) — Exploring the Missing Link Between the Concussion "Crisis" and Labor Politics in [Professional Sports]

**Core Argument:** Media coverage of sports concussions frames the issue as a medical/safety crisis while obscuring labor politics. Working conditions (including injury risk) are inseparable from athlete compensation and collective bargaining.

## Data Context for Chapter

### League Revenue & Stability (2018-24)
- Attendance recovery pattern post-COVID tracks broader league economic resilience
- TV revenue growth ($24B → $76B deal) reflects negotiating power dynamics
- Connection: Revenue strength determines management's capacity to resist labor demands

### Attendance Dynamics
- **2018-19:** 21.96M fans — league at near-record; labor landscape stable
- **2019-20:** Bubble (zero attendance) — unprecedented disruption; labor negotiations froze
- **2020-21:** 671K fans (96% drop) — maximum economic pressure on both sides
- **2021-22:** First full post-COVID season; recovery trajectory (official data missing)
- **2022-23:** 22.23M fans — all-time record; league strength restored before 2023 labor negotiations

### Why This Matters
Revenue trajectories and attendance patterns show when labor has leverage and when management does. The concussion issue surfaces most visibly during periods of high league profitability and player negotiating strength—not during crisis periods. Brayton's argument about media erasure of labor politics can be grounded in these timelines.
