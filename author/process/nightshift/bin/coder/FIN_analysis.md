# FIN: NBA Franchise Valuation and Operating Income

## Franchise Valuations (Forbes-Based)

### Data Source and Methodology
- **Primary source:** Forbes.com annual NBA team valuations
- Operationalized as the **year-over-year change in organizational brand value** (incremental value)
- Coverage: Six seasons (2015–16 through 2020–21), all 30 NBA teams (n=180 team-seasons) [S1]

### Key Valuations
- **Golden State Warriors** identified as the most valuable franchise (Zaharia 2026) [S3]
- Press release reference: "Forbes releases 21st annual NBA team valuations" (Feb 6, 2019) [S6]

### Operating Income and Revenue Streams
Kim et al. (2024) report [S1]:
- **Total revenue** (gathered from runrepeat.com)
- **Ticket revenue** (gathered from runrepeat.com)
- **Operating income** (gathered from runrepeat.com)

Gonsalves (2022) identifies revenue sources [S2]:
- Ticket sales
- Premium seating
- Concessions
- Merchandise
- Naming rights
- Local TV/radio deals

### Revenue Scale (Sample)
Gonsalves (2022) average revenue by diversity scorecard tier (single year-over-year, 2020→2021) [S2]:
- Score 2: **$214M**
- Score 3: **$218M**
- Score 4: **$197M**

### Media Rights Impact on Valuations
Bukstein & Eisenberg (n.d.):
- **2014 nine-year media deal** (Turner/ESPN, $24B total)
- Raised per-team media revenue from **~$30M to over $75M annually** starting 2016–17
- Generated "strong revenue growth and significant increases in franchise values" in preceding three years [S5]
