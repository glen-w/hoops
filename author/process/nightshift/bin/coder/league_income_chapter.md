# League Income: Media Rights, Salary Cap, and Revenue

## Media Rights Agreements

### 2016–2024-25 Cycle
The NBA's prior broadcast agreement (2016-17 through 2024-25) partnered with **ABC/ESPN** and **TNT/Turner** (both part of Turner Broadcasting System under The Walt Disney Company). The nine-year agreement covered multiple telecast windows per week, with an estimated total value of approximately **$24 billion** over the period, or **$2.6 billion per year** on average.[^1][^2]

This deal marked a significant pivot from the previous media era, doubling per-team annual television revenue and establishing dual primetime partners (Disney and Turner) as structural anchors.

### 2025–2035-36 Cycle
Beginning 2025-26, the NBA expanded its broadcast partnership to three parties: **ABC/ESPN (Disney), NBC/Peacock (NBCUniversal), and Amazon Prime Video**. The new agreement spans ten seasons with a total contract value of approximately **$77 billion**, or roughly **$6.9 billion per year** on average—a 165% increase from the prior cycle.[^3][^4]

| Partner | Annual Value (USD) | Source |
|---------|-------------------|--------|
| Disney (ABC/ESPN) | $2,620,000,000 | CNBC 2024-07-24 |
| NBCUniversal (NBC/Peacock) | $2,450,000,000 | CNBC 2024-07-24 |
| Amazon Prime Video | $1,800,000,000 | CNBC 2024-07-24 |

Per-team national TV revenue increases from approximately $103 million (2024-25) to $143 million (2025-26) under the new agreements.[^5]

## Salary Cap and Team Payroll Thresholds

The NBA salary cap—the ceiling for guaranteed player salaries per team—is set annually by league and players' union negotiation, based on projected basketball-related income (BRI). Official salary cap figures (HIGH confidence) from NBA Communications releases:[^6][^7][^8][^9]

| Season | Salary Cap (USD) | Luxury Tax Threshold (USD) | First Apron (USD) | Second Apron (USD) |
|--------|------------------|--------------------------|-------------------|-------------------|
| 2022-23 | 123,655,000 | 150,267,000 | — | — |
| 2023-24 | 136,021,000 | 165,294,000 | — | — |
| 2024-25 | 140,588,000 | 170,814,000 | — | — |
| 2025-26 | 154,647,000 | 187,895,000 | 195,945,000 | 207,824,000 |

Beginning with the 2025-26 season, the CBA introduced two new apron levels to restrict financial flexibility for teams above the tax threshold, part of a broader effort to police competitive balance.

## Revenue Totals and Data Gaps

Basketball-related income (BRI)—the pool of shared revenue used to calculate salary cap—totaled approximately **$10.25 billion** in 2024-25, with league and team revenues (excluding shared portions) at roughly **$12.75 billion**. Projections for 2025-26 suggest gross league revenue will reach **$14.3 billion**, reflecting media-rights increases and playoff revenue growth.[^10]

### Known Data Gaps
- **Licensing and merchandise revenue:** No comprehensive league-wide audited figure is publicly available.
- **Arena and ticket revenue:** Individual team disclosures appear in SEC filings (MSG Sports, selected owners), but league-wide totals remain private.
- **Local media and sponsorship:** Highly variable by market and team; no consolidated league total exists in public sources.

These gaps reflect the NBA's limited financial transparency outside official salary-cap disclosures and partner announcements.
