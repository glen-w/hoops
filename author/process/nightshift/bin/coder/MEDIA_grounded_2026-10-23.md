## 1. National TV Rights: Six Resets, 1990–2026

### Broadcast → Cable (1990–2002): NBC era, $150M → $485M/yr
- **1990-94:** NBC $600M (4 yr), Turner $275M (medium confidence, Variety 1997 recap)
- **1994-98:** NBC $750M + revenue sharing; Turner ~$350M
- **1998-2002:** NBC $1.75B (4 yr; ~$437M/yr); Turner $890M (~$222M/yr)
- *Frame*: Network broadcast peak, then cable begins to climb

### Cable Mainstream (2002–2008): ABC/ESPN vs Turner, ~$765M/yr total
- **2002-08:** Disney (ABC/ESPN) $2.4B (6 yr; ~$400M/yr); Turner $2.2B (~$367M/yr)
- *Shift*: NBC exits after 12 seasons; finals move to ABC; most games shift to cable
- *New angle*: Deal bundles **digital, broadband, mobile** rights (first time)

### Cable Growth (2008–2016): Disney–Turner duopoly, $930M → $1.3B/yr
- **2008-16:** Disney (ESPN) ~$485M/yr; Turner ~$445M/yr (rough WSJ split)
- Includes **NBA TV, NBA.com, League Pass** turned over to Turner (2008), profit-sharing venture
- Escalator: not found for any deal

### Current: Three-Partner Reset (2025-26 to 2035-36), $6.9B/yr
- **Reported annual values** (cross-checked SBJ/Reuters/AP, not NBA-confirmed):
  - Disney (ESPN/ABC) ~$2.6B/yr
  - NBCUniversal (NBC/Peacock) ~$2.5B/yr
  - Amazon Prime Video ~$1.8B/yr
  - **Total ~$76–77B over 11 years**
- **Game counts:** Broadcast TV up to ~75 games/yr (from ~15 floor); Disney 80, NBC up to 100, Amazon 66 + NBA Cup + Play-In
- **Amazon global League Pass:** third-party home for out-of-market 2025-26+
- **TNT/WBD exit:** tried to match Amazon's $1.8B, deal didn't match terms per NBA. WBD sued NY state court.
- **WBD settlement (18 Nov 2024):** 11-yr partnership, live intl rights (Nordics, Poland, Lat Am ex Brazil/Mexico), highlights/content for B/R. **Inside the NBA** stays TNT-produced, airs on ESPN/ABC from 2025-26.
- **NBA TV in-house (1 Oct 2025):** ~60 games, end of Turner partnership

## 2. Regional Sports Networks: Boom, Consolidation, Collapse (2019–2026)

### Consolidation era: Sinclair buys Fox RSNs (2019)
- **23 Aug 2019:** Sinclair acquires 21 ex-Fox RSNs from Disney, $10.6B enterprise value ($9.6B cash)
- Rebranded **Bally Sports** 2021

### Bankruptcy and Exit Cascade: Diamond Sports Group → Main Street (2023–2026)
- **14 Mar 2023:** Diamond Sports Group (19 networks, 42 teams, 16 NBA teams) files Chapter 11, $8B+ debt
- **2023-24 first exits:**
  - Suns → Gray TV + Kiswe streaming (bankruptcy judge voided deal May 2023; Diamond declined to match)
  - Jazz → KJZZ + Jazz+ app
  - Rockets → Space City Home Network (team/Astros bought AT&T SportsNet Southwest, 3 Oct 2023)
- **Comcast carriage blackout:** Bally off Xfinity 1 May–29 Jul 2024 (mostly MLB, end of NBA playoffs affected)
- **2024-25 wave:**
  - Mavericks → TEGNA (WFAA/KFAA) + MavsTV+
  - Pelicans → Gray Media + Pelicans+
  - Trail Blazers → KATU/KUNP + BlazerVision
  - SBJ count: 13 NBA teams remain on over-the-air + traditional RSNs by Oct 2024
- **21 Oct 2024:** Bally rebranded **FanDuel Sports Network**
- **2 Jan 2025:** Diamond emerges as **Main Street Sports Group** (16 FDSN networks, 13 NBA teams: Hawks, Hornets, Cavaliers, Pistons, Pacers, Clippers, Grizzlies, Heat, Bucks, Timberwolves, Thunder, Magic, Spurs)
- **Apr 2026 wind-down:** Main Street ends NBA coverage end-of-regular-season (12 Apr 2026). No rights fees 2026; teams can recover ~60%. 13 teams freed for new deals 2026-27. NBA planning national streaming "local hub" (2027-28+).

### 2026-27 RSN map (post-Main Street)
- **DAZN exclusive (5 teams):** Pacers, Cavaliers, Timberwolves, Grizzlies, Spurs (15–22 games/yr simulcast OTA)
- **DAZN + local OTA (3):** Hornets (Cox WSOC/TV64), Pistons (Scripps "The Spot"), Magic (Cox/WFTV)
- **OTA + team app or Prime (5):** Hawks (Gray Peachtree + Prime in-market), Heat (WPLG), Bucks (MY24 + Bucks+), Thunder (Griffin + Thunder+), Clippers (ClipperVision + KTLA)
- **Traditional RSNs still live (15):** NBC regionals (Celtics, Warriors, 76ers, Kings), YES (Nets), MSG (Knicks), Altitude (Nuggets), Spectrum SportsNet (Lakers), CHSN (Bulls), Monumental (Wizards), Space City (Rockets), TSN/Sportsnet (Raptors)

## 4. International: Tencent and China (2015–2026)

### Digital boom and political collapse (2015–2020)
- **30 Jan 2015:** Tencent 5-yr digital deal (from Jul 2015), reported ~$500M
- **29 Jul 2019:** 5-yr expansion through 2024-25, reported $1.5B
- **Oct 2019 Morey Hong Kong tweet:** Tencent suspends NBA preseason; some resume within days. State broadcaster CCTV keeps NBA off air until 10 Oct 2020 (Finals Game 5). Silver estimated loss "hundreds of millions," likely <$400M.
- **Oct 2024:** Tencent renews as exclusive digital partner (through ~2027; value not public)
- **2025:** China Games return

### Three-tier frame (international stratification)
- **Top tier:** national US package, rising from ~$900M/yr (2008) → ~$2.6B/yr (2016) → ~$6.9B/yr (2025)
- **Middle tier:** local RSN money, collapsed 2023–2026
- **Bottom tier:** international digital (Tencent), subject to political suspension without warning

## 6. Caveats and What's Uncertain

- **Dollar amounts:** NBA almost never publishes figures. Nearly all come from press (SBJ, NYT, Reuters, LA Times) and should cite as "reported"
- **Escalator schedules:** per-season numbers are simple averages; no season-by-season fee schedules found for any deal
- **Tencent:** 2015/2019 values reported; 2024 renewal value unknown; end season uncertain (2026-27 or 2027-28)
- **Historical turnover:** 1990-94 and 1994-98 Turner totals not opened against 1989 primary sources; Bally rebrand date not confirmed
