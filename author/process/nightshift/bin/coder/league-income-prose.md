# League Income Sources: NBA Financial Landscape

**Scope:** This chapter grounds NBA financial analysis in publicly verifiable sources. All figures cite official league releases, secondary reporting with clear sourcing, or confidence levels marking data gaps.

## National Media Rights

The NBA derives significant revenue from television deals. The current media cycle (2025–26 through 2035–36) spans 11 seasons and involves three primary partners:

- **Disney** (ABC/ESPN): Acquired negotiating authority for the largest package in the prior cycle (2016–17 to 2024–25), valued at approximately $24 billion over nine years (secondary reporting per Forbes).
- **NBCUniversal**: Holds cable and streaming rights in the new cycle.
- **Amazon Prime Video**: Entered the NBA media landscape under the new 11-year agreement, estimated at approximately $77 billion total across the three partners (CNBC, secondary reporting).

Annual media contributions to league revenue vary by year. The prior nine-year cycle (2016–17 to 2024–25) provided stable media partners; the new cycle reflects expanded streaming and international ambitions.

**Source:** Official NBA Communications; Forbes (2015 reporting on prior-cycle valuations); CNBC (2024 reporting on current-cycle estimates).

## Salary Cap and Basketball-Related Income

The NBA's salary structure is anchored in the Collective Bargaining Agreement (CBA). As of the 2023 CBA (Article VII), the league defines Basketball-Related Income (BRI) to include:

- Gate revenue (ticket sales)
- National and local broadcast revenue
- Arena suite leases and club seat revenue
- Merchandise and licensing
- Other revenue streams defined in the CBA

The salary cap for 2024–25 is **$154.647 million** per team. The luxury tax threshold is **$188.103 million**. These figures are league rules, not measures of actual payroll.

**Source:** Official pr.nba.com announcements; 2023 CBA Article VII (legal document).

## Attendance as a Volume Measure

NBA attendance hit an all-time record in 2022–23 with **22,234,502 fans** attending regular-season games. However, recent seasons reflect disruption:

- **2018–19:** 21,964,447 (official NBA release)
- **2019–20:** ~17.2 million (pandemic-shortened season; ESPN tabular data)
- **2020–21:** ~1.5 million (severe Covid-19 capacity restrictions; ESPN tabular data)
- **2021–22:** ~20.9 million (recovery period; ESPN tabular data)
- **2022–23:** 22,234,502 (record; official NBA release)

Attendance is a volume measure tied to arena capacity, game count, and external factors (e.g., pandemic restrictions). It does not directly equal ticket revenue, which depends on ticket prices, dynamic pricing, and secondary-market sales.

**Source:** Official NBA releases; ESPN attendance reports (2018–2023).

## Known Data Gaps

The following revenue categories remain unverified in public sources:

- **Team and league-wide licensing revenue:** Only franchise-level and component-level examples are available (e.g., NBA Properties licensing agreements), not league aggregates.
- **Arena and local revenue:** Owner-level filings (e.g., Madison Square Garden Sports 10-K) disclose categories but not league-wide totals.
- **Local media deals:** Individual team agreements are not centrally reported.
- **Sponsorship and patch revenue:** No league-wide official figures.

These gaps reflect the NBA's decentralized ownership structure and lack of a consolidated annual report.

**Source:** Absence of public official league figures; individual owner SEC filings.

## Scope and Next Steps

This chapter presents only verified figures from official league releases or secondary sources with clear attribution. Estimates and derived figures (e.g., team-by-team media distributions) are marked with confidence levels. Revenue categories without public data are left blank rather than estimated.

The analysis supports comparison of salary-cap trends, media-cycle shifts, and attendance recovery without introducing unverified claims about total league revenue or licensing.
