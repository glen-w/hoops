## The Current State of Media in the NBA

The excerpts paint a picture of the NBA as a league that has undergone a profound digital and media transformation, positioning itself as a global entertainment and media powerhouse rather than solely a sports organization.

### Digital Business Model and Revenue

The NBA operates what Santomier et al. describe as a "digitally focused hybrid business model" characterized by a complex web of products, services, and distribution channels [S3]. Much of the league's revenue is categorized as Basketball Related Income (BRI), including ticketing, concessions, TV contracts, and merchandising rights [S3]. Neuhaus & Thomas note that the NBA has been "at the forefront of global media-driven financialization," using personality-driven storytelling and what they call the "Disneynization of the sport" to cement its position in people's lives [S2]. Despite falling television ratings, the NBA has continued to grow its media income by capturing attention through social media and experimenting with emerging technologies like the Metaverse [S2].

### Social Media and Player Voice

Social media has become central to the NBA's media ecosystem. Manning et al. note that players now have significantly more control over their image and voice through social media and digital outlets, where they share daily lives, off-court work, cultural brands, and political viewpoints [S1]. Huyghe reports that 79% of NBA players had a Twitter account between 2012 and 2015, and that social media has "clearly grown into an inseparable part of the modern NBA player's lifestyle" [S7]. The league's own digital footprint is substantial: the term "NBA" was the fourth most Googled term in 2021, with 24 billion views and 2.3 billion engagements across official social media channels (Facebook, Instagram, Snapchat) [S2].

### Content Delivery and Technology

The NBA has multiplied the ways fans can engage with the sport. Santomier et al. detail products including NBA TV (live games, studio programming, on-demand video), an OTT streaming channel, NBA TV Center Court (with smartphone-optimized camera angles, live on-screen group chats, integrated analytics, and social media integration), the Second Spectrum player-tracking system, and Mobile View for smaller screens [S3].
