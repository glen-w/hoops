# Broadcast regionalism and competitive inequality, 1950–1980

The structural inequality in NBA broadcast access shaped franchise competitive balance more directly than previously acknowledged. The Los Angeles Lakers' regional media partnership with broadcaster Chick Hearn (radio beginning 1956, television 1961) created a geographic reach extending across Southern California, generating fan engagement and sponsorship revenue unavailable to smaller-market franchises. Similarly, the Boston Celtics' Boston-area broadcast dominance (with Johnny Most on radio) and the New York Knicks' Madison Square Garden visibility established regional media moats. Team-owned and regional syndication networks, distinct from later NBA national contracts (1962 onward), embedded revenue and visibility advantages that correlated directly with on-court success. Regional broadcast advantage enabled franchise stability and competitiveness; this media-driven inequality persisted even after the NBA centralized television rights in the 1960s, as local broadcast revenue and audience depth remained structurally favored for large-market franchises. Understanding broadcast regionalism is essential to explaining persistent NBA dominance by media-advantaged franchises and the competitive disadvantage of smaller-market teams.
