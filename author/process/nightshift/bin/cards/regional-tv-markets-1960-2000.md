# Regional & Local Television Markets in Professional Basketball, 1960–2000

{Regional TV Markets, 1960-2000; PENDING}

From the 1960s through the 1990s, NBA franchises relied on a patchwork of regional and local television contracts rather than a single national package. Early deals were often brokered by individual teams with independent stations or regional sports networks (RSNs) such as SportsChannel, Prime Network, and later Fox Sports Net. Revenue sharing between national (CBS, NBC) and local broadcasts was minimal until the 1990s collective bargaining agreements formalised league-wide pooling. Local market size dictated rights fees: New York, Los Angeles, and Chicago commanded premiums 5–10× smaller markets. The 1980s cable boom accelerated RSN development; teams like the Lakers and Celtics pioneered team-owned production units. Scholarly work by Fort & Quirk (1995) on sports broadcasting economics and league documents from the NBA Archive (1984–1998) detail fee structures. Primary sources include FCC filings, RSN carriage agreements, and NBA Board of Governors minutes (1970–2000). Gaps remain in granular per-season local rights fees for 1960–1980 and the precise revenue-split formulas pre-1995.
