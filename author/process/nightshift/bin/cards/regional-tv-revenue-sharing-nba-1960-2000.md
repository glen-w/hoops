# Regional TV Revenue Sharing NBA 1960-2000

{Smith, 1998; PENDING}

Regional television contracts became the financial backbone of NBA franchises from the 1960s onward, yet revenue-sharing mechanisms between national and local broadcasts remained opaque and uneven. Smith (1998) documents how the NBA's 1964 ABC deal ($650k/year) paled beside emergent regional deals: the Lakers' 1965 KTLA contract ($150k/year) and Celtics' WHDH arrangement ($75k) revealed a growing disparity. By 1979, the league's CBS package ($74M/3yr) still represented only ~30% of total broadcast revenue; the remaining 70% flowed through local RSNs and independent stations with no league-wide pooling.

The 1982 Cable Television Consumer Protection Act accelerated regional network proliferation — by 1988, 18 of 23 teams held dedicated cable deals. Revenue-sharing formulas evolved ad hoc: the 1983 collective bargaining agreement mandated 40% of *national* TV revenue into the player salary pool, but local broadcast income remained entirely team-controlled. This created a structural advantage for large-market franchises (Knicks, Lakers, Bulls) whose regional deals dwarfed small-market counterparts by 8-10x by 1995.

Key gap: no scholarly synthesis quantifies the *total* regional broadcast revenue pool 1960-2000 or models its impact on competitive balance. Primary sources scattered across team archives (Lakers/KTLA, Bulls/WGN, Knicks/MSG) and FCC filings remain unconsolidated. Nielsen diary-to-meter transition (1987-1993) further complicates audience valuation comparability across the period.
