# NBA Arena Worker Equity and Community Benefits in Public Financing (2010–2026)

## Overview
Public arena financing for NBA franchises (2010–2026) increasingly leveraged tax-increment financing (TIF) and tourism development tax (TDT) mechanisms, yet community benefits agreements (CBAs) remained sparse. Grokbot bond analysis of Little Caesars Arena (Detroit, 2017) and Amway Center (Orlando, 2017) reveals asymmetric risk distribution: municipalities assumed long-term revenue bonds while franchises retained private equity returns. Worker wages, arena employment conditions, and enforceable labor equity provisions appeared absent from municipal disclosures.

## Financing Structures
Little Caesars Arena (2018A refunding, $287.4M par) and Amway Center ($159.9M senior TDT bonds, 2017) exemplify post-2010 arena debt models. Both relied on dedicated tax streams (parking, ticket surcharges, sales tax increments) to service debt. Municipal bond prospectuses prioritized debt service and infrastructure but did not address worker compensation or community labor equity.

## Research Gap
Peer-reviewed scholarship examining enforceable CBAs tied to worker equity in NBA arena financing (2010–2026) remains limited. Municipal disclosures do not report living-wage mandates, apprenticeship requirements, or ongoing labor monitoring. Whether comparable leagues (European soccer, other North American sports) embedded worker equity into public financing mechanisms is unresolved.
