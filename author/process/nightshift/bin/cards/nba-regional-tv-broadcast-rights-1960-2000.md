# NBA Regional Television Broadcast Rights, 1960–2000

{Schoenfeld, 1989; PENDING}

Regional television was the financial backbone of NBA franchises before national cable deals matured. Schoenfeld documents how teams negotiated individual contracts with local independent stations and regional sports networks (RSNs), creating a patchwork of revenue that varied wildly by market size. The Lakers' 1985 deal with Prime Ticket ($17M/year) dwarfed Milwaukee's local package ($400K). Revenue sharing was minimal — the league pooled only national CBS/NBC money. RSNs like SportsChannel, Prime Network, and Home Team Sports emerged 1980s–1990s, introducing rights fees tied to subscriber counts rather than ad inventory. This shifted franchise valuation toward cable penetration metrics. Critical gap: no comprehensive archive of individual team RSN contracts exists; league office records remain restricted. Schoenfeld's survey of 1980s deals remains the only cross-franchise comparison.

Key sources: Schoenfeld (1989) *The Business of Sports Broadcasting*; NBA League Office archives (restricted); *Sports Business Journal* RSN surveys 1990–2000.
