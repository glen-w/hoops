# Arena Labor Equity: Absent from Contemporary Financing Structures

Community benefits agreements and worker equity provisions are conspicuously absent from contemporary NBA arena financing mechanisms. Tax-increment financing (TIF, Detroit's Little Caesars Arena) and tourism development tax (TDT, Orlando's Amway Center) shift public financial risk to private franchises, yet bond prospectuses and governance documents contain no enforceable commitments to worker wage standards, union contracts, or community jobs guarantees.

Arena construction and operations employ thousands—security, food service, maintenance, ushers—yet these workers are systematically excluded from benefit-sharing in public-private financing structures. Franchise owners accumulate equity through publicly-backed debt instruments while workers absorb wage volatility and employment precarity.
