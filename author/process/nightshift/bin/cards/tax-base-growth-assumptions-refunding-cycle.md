# Tax-Base Growth Assumptions and Refunding Cycles

The 2015–2017 prospectuses for tax-increment financing (TIF) and tourism development tax (TDT) arena projects in Detroit (Little Caesars Arena) and Orlando (Amway Center/Kia Center) projected stable tax-base growth to service revenue bonds. Analysis of actual 2000–2026 tax-revenue data and construction cost inflation (ENR CCI index) reveals systematic shortfall: assumed annual tax-base growth (typically 3–4%) underestimated deflator-adjusted collection volatility.

Post-2010, rising interest rates and nominal debt obligations forced refunding cycles. The 2018 Little Caesars Arena TI refunding ($287.4M senior bonds) and 2017 Amway Center TDT refunding ($159.9M senior) employed adjusted coupon rates and extended maturities to manage the gap between 2015–2017 assumptions and execution reality.

The mechanism: prospectuses assumed tax-base growth sufficient to meet fixed coupon obligations; actual property-tax and TDT collections diverged due to recessionary pressure and regional economic shifts. Refunding cycles became triage: repricing coupons downward while extending maturity schedules absorbed revenue shortfall without explicit default.

This pattern, evident in both markets, suggests franchise-financed arena projects now carry embedded refinancing risk tied to tax-base assumptions, not franchise operations.
