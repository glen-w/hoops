# NBA Arena Senior Debt Structures: Public-Private Risk Distribution in Post-2010 Financing

The 2026-11-01 Grokbot analysis of Little Caesars Arena (Detroit, 2018) and Amway Center / Kia Center (Orlando, 2017) illustrates post-2010 institutional patterns. Little Caesars featured $287.4M in 2018A tax-increment financing (TIF) senior refunding bonds; Amway Center issued $159.9M in Tourism Development Tax (TDT) senior refunding revenue bonds. Both employ dedicated tax streams—TIF capturing property tax increments, TDT diverting hospitality taxes—to back repayment while insulating public general funds from construction costs.

Post-2010 arena finance created specialized risk distribution: franchises and municipalities collaborated to lever predictable revenues (tickets, naming rights, concessions) into debt service. This contrasts with earlier public-financed arenas: revenue bonds explicitly tied repayment to arena performance rather than general municipal credit. These structures became institutional templates enabling franchise modernization while distributing financial risk between public authorities (tax commitment, oversight) and private operators (revenue generation, management).
