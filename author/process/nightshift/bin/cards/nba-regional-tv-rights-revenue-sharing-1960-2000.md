# NBA Regional TV Rights & Revenue Sharing, 1960–2000

{Szymanski, 2003}; {Fort, 2000}; {Quirk & Fort, 1997}; {Noll, 2007}; {Leeds & von Allmen, 2008}; {NBA League Office Archives, 1960–2000}; {Regional Sports Network Filings, 1980–2000}; {Broadcasting & Cable Yearbooks, 1960–2000}

Regional television rights became the financial backbone of NBA franchises from the 1980s onward. Early national contracts (ABC, CBS) paid modest fees — $1.5M–$5M annually league-wide — while local stations paid $50K–$500K per team. The 1982 CBS deal ($28M/2yr) still left most revenue to gate receipts. Cable changed everything: Ted Turner's WTBS (1976) and later regional sports networks (PRISM 1976, SportsChannel 1979, Prime Network 1988, Fox Sports Net 1996) created competitive local markets. By 1998, local TV rights averaged $8–12M per franchise; Knicks/Lakers commanded $25M+. Revenue sharing remained minimal — national pool split equally, local rights kept 100% by franchise. The 1995 collective bargaining agreement formalised a 40/60 split (players/owners) of *defined* basketball-related income, but local broadcast revenue definition stayed contested. Franchises in top-10 markets (NY, LA, Chicago) built war chests; small-market teams (Milwaukee, Sacramento) fell behind. Scholarly consensus: regional TV rights drove the franchise valuation gap from 3:1 (1980) to 15:1 (2000). Primary gaps: confidential RSN carriage agreements, side-letter revenue definitions, and pre-1980 local station contracts remain largely unarchived.
