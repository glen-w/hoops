# Analytics & Statistical Analysis in Professional Basketball, 1950–2000

{Szymanski, 2003; PENDING} — Szymanski traces the migration of quantitative methods from baseball into basketball, noting that the NBA’s official statistics remained confined to basic box-score categories (points, rebounds, assists) until the 1970s. Efficiency metrics such as points per possession and adjusted plus-minus emerged only in academic working papers after 1990.

{Oliver, 2004; PENDING} — Dean Oliver’s *Basketball on Paper* codified the “Four Factors” (effective field-goal percentage, turnover rate, offensive-rebound rate, free-throw rate) and introduced offensive/defensive ratings per 100 possessions. Oliver documents limited team adoption before 2000; the Dallas Mavericks and Seattle SuperSonics experimented with possession-based scouting in the late 1990s.

{Kubatko et al., 2007; PENDING} — Retrospective study confirms that league-wide analytics infrastructure (play-by-play databases, video tracking) did not exist before the 1996–97 season. Early pioneers (e.g., Martin Manley, Roland Beech) operated outside team structures, publishing on Usenet and early websites.

Primary gap: no scholarly monograph covers 1950–1990 statistical evolution; surviving sources are league yearbooks, *The Sporting News* archives, and team media guides. Zotero keys pending human supply.
