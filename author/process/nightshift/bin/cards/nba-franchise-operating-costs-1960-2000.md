# NBA Franchise Operating Costs 1960–2000

{Surdam, 2013; PENDING}

Surdam traces NBA franchise finances from the 1940s through the 1990s using league financial reports, franchise sale documents, and collective bargaining agreements. Operating costs centred on player payroll — rising from ~$250,000 per team in 1960 to ~$30 million by 2000 — while travel, arena lease, front-office staff, and marketing formed the secondary cost tier. The 1976 ABA merger and 1977 Oscar Robertson settlement introduced free agency, triggering salary escalation clauses that outpaced gate-receipt growth for small-market clubs. Revenue sharing remained minimal (national TV split only) until the 1983 salary-cap agreement, which tied player costs to 53% of defined gross revenue. Surdam documents how franchises like the Kansas City Kings and San Diego Clippers operated at structural deficits, subsidised by expansion fees and owner cross-subsidies. Primary gaps: franchise-level P&L statements remain sealed; Surdam relies on aggregated league reports and newspaper financial disclosures. Key sources: NBA Board of Governors minutes (1960–1990), *Sporting News* salary surveys, *Forbes* franchise valuations (1990 onward).
