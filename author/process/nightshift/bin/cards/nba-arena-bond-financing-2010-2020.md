# NBA Arena Capital Finance & Bond Structures (2010–2020)

Modern NBA arenas rely on municipal bonds for $400M–$1B+ construction. Little Caesars Arena (Detroit, 2017 opening, ~$750M) issued senior TI refunding bonds ($287.4M par) in 2018, reflecting debt-service obligations tied to team revenue. Amway Center (Kia Center, Orlando, 2019 opening, ~$555M) issued senior TDT refunding bonds ($159.9M par, structured around 6th Cent Contract Payments) in 2017. Both leveraged city-backed revenue pledges—ticket sales, concessions, naming rights—to service long-term debt.

ENR Construction Cost Index (1913=100) reveals building-cost inflation: 6,221 (2000), rising to ~7,250 (2026), outpacing general CPI. This escalation increases capital requirements and refinancing pressure. Bond disclosure documents (Official Statements, Debt Service Schedules) expose franchises to revenue-shortfall risk and market-viability constraints. Arena financing became integral to competitive durability: local tax base, debt-service coverage, and market demographics shaped franchise location decisions and financial capacity 2010–2020.
