**Venue Economics and Franchise Viability, 1950–2000**

Jozsa (1999; 6WT9ESIJ) examined metropolitan-area statistics—population, income, attendance, franchise values—to assess professional sports business decisions and franchise viability across the 1950–2000 period. Downs & Seifried (2023; FPYIXZPD) situate professional basketball facilities within capitalist frameworks, noting that by the late 1970s the NBA had expanded to 22 franchises through strategies of retrenchment, relocation, and expansion. The Milwaukee Arena (1950), an 11,046-seat facility costing approximately $6 million, exemplifies the municipal-funding model adopted across many cities. Conversely, the WHA and ABA's reliance on small arenas produced insufficient revenues, contributing to their decline. Empirical research identified that newly constructed or renovated arenas correlated with increased attendance. Collectively, these sources establish that facility capacity and local market demographics functioned as primary determinants of franchise revenue potential and competitive longevity during professional basketball's formative decades.
