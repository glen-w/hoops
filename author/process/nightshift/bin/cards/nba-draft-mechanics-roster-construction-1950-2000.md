# NBA Draft Mechanics and Roster Construction (1950–2000)

Professional basketball teams constructed competitive rosters primarily through the NBA draft, which underwent three major regulatory transformations. Before 1966, territorial picks allowed franchises to select college players from their immediate region to cultivate local fanbases (N3MUFBVX). The league abolished this system in 1966, distributing first-round picks via coin flip between last-place conference finishers and allocating remaining selections inversely by record (N3MUFBVX). By 1985, the NBA introduced a lottery system to eliminate incentive for deliberate tanking, granting all seven non-playoff teams equal odds for the first pick (N3MUFBVX).

Draft selection carried measurable cost: each position commanded substantially different salary expectations, reflecting a team's capital investment in that player's potential (Staw & Hoang, 1995, LC9LG8BW). Professional scouting departments expanded to review NBA rosters, minor-league players, college talent, secondary schools, and international prospects (Wong & Deubert, 2013, 8UMLVJLE). The average NBA career spanned four to five years, forcing constant roster turnover and systematic talent replacement (Wong & Deubert, 2013, 8UMLVJLE). After FIBA eliminated amateur/professional distinctions in 1989, international players began reshaping roster composition, initially sparse but accelerating towards 2000 (Eschker et al., 2004, TGEYDGGR).
