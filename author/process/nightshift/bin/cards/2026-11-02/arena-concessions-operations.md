# Arena Concessions: Vendor Consolidation and Franchise Revenue

Aramark, Levy Restaurants, and similar vendors operate the majority of NBA arena concessions, from food service to merchandise logistics. This consolidation reflects broader outsourcing trends in stadium operations. Vendor agreements structure revenue-sharing between franchises and concessionaires: franchises typically secure 20–40% of gross revenues whilst vendors absorb operational risk and labour costs. This arrangement shifts wage and scheduling pressures onto vendors and their workforce. The vendor model permits franchises to stabilize concessions revenue independent of demand volatility—a critical advantage when financing new arenas through debt projections. Consolidation also standardizes menus and pricing across regions, though major-market franchises often negotiate superior terms. The shift from independent concessionaires (1950s–1980s) to corporate consolidation (1990s onward) reflects franchises' strategy to professionalize operations and capture consumer behaviour data. Recent scholarship examines how vendor consolidation affects both franchise margins and workforce precarity in facility services.
