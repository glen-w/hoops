**Pricing and Accessibility in NBA Arena Concessions**

Premium seating commands premium concessions. Luxury suites and club sections offer curated menus, table service, and price points far beyond general-admission options. Yet the broader economic logic remains opaque: how do franchise food systems reflect and reinforce stadium stratification?

Concession pricing in arenas follows two distinct markets. Suite and premium-seat patrons access higher-margin beverages, prepared meals, and specialty items; general-concourse visitors face limited inventory at marked-up prices, constrained by queue density and layout. A foundational question: are these structures driven by cost-of-goods, labour, or captive-market extraction?

Vendor consolidation—Aramark, Levy Restaurants, and Sports Service Enterprises dominate NBA arena food operations—centralises procurement and labour control. This concentration enables franchise profitability but obscures worker equity and fan affordability. Dietary inclusion, family access, and equitable queuing remain unmapped in scholarly literature.

What would equitable arena food access entail? How do architectural choices—concourse width, seating isolation, queue design—enable or obstruct fair pricing and accessibility across seating tiers?
