Arena concessions: the untold profit driver in franchise finance. At £12–£18 per patron, beer and nachos don't read as serious business—yet a 20,000-seat venue pulls £240,000+ per game on food service alone. Scholarship overlooks it entirely. Why? Because concessions get outsourced (Aramark, Compass dominate the market) or run in-house; either way, the revenue split shapes franchise profitability in ways arena finance scholarship simply ignores. Luxury suite catering commands premium margins on premium pricing. Beverage sponsorships reshape vendor models. Structural decisions—whether concessions stay in-house or get contracted out—produce wildly different profit pictures and valuation implications. Franchise finance papers fixate on naming rights, salary-cap luxury tax, premium seating. Food service becomes operational overhead, merely incidental. Yet for ownership accumulation and franchise valuation growth, concessions matter substantially more than scholarship admits.
