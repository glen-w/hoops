# NBA Fan Cost and Concession Pricing: Aggregation and Opacity

**Santomier *et al.* (2023)** reported the NBA Fan Cost Index (FCI)—the total outlay for a family of four—at US$430.25 for the 2019–20 season, bundling tickets, concessions, and parking into a single metric. The concession component remains opaque in league reporting; Paperful sources offer only fragmentary data. **Downs & Seifried (2023)** unearthed 1970s Pittsburgh Civic Arena concession pricing: hot dogs from $0.45, sandwiches $0.95–$1.00, sugary items $0.25. Adjusted to 2022 inflation, these suggest deliberate price stratification targeting "middle class adults, children, and corporate customers," yet no scholarly study systematically examines NBA concession pricing equity, dietary accessibility, or how service-vendor consolidation (Aramark, Levy) has reshaped affordability since 1980. The FCI aggregates concessions into the broader fan-cost burden, but league data obscures the concession-to-ticket ratio and pricing's relationship to fan retention and demographic access.
