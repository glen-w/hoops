# Vendor Labour in Professional Sports Arenas

**Downs & Seifried (2023)** examine how major food-service vendors operating across NFL and NBA venues navigate unionised and non-unionised labour markets. Their analysis reveals structural tensions: franchises outsource catering and concessions to firms like Aramark and Levy, which absorb labour-relations risk. The **2004 CBA**, whilst silent on vendor operations, establishes wage floors and dispute resolution for players—a contrast highlighting the absence of labour protections for ground-crew and food-service workers. Collective bargaining in professional sports arenas remains fragmentary, with regional vendor contracts often featuring minimal wage standards and rotating seasonal staff. This labour precarity directly shapes in-suite and premium-seating operations, where vendor margins depend on staffing elasticity rather than stability or craft. The result: high-turnover, low-skill positioning, and pressure on food quality and service consistency—particularly in luxury suites where franchise revenue expectations exceed what fragmented vendor labour can reliably deliver.
