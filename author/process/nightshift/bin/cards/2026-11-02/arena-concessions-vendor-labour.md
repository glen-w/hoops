# Arena Food Concessions: Vendor Consolidation & Labour Equity

Aramark, Levy Restaurants, and Sports Service Enterprises operate the vast majority of NBA arena food concessions, from concourse retail to luxury-suite catering. Revenue-sharing typically grants teams 30–50% of concession sales; vendors absorb labour costs and operations. This model creates structural distance: concession workers are vendor employees, not franchise staff, limiting franchise accountability for wages and conditions.

Vendor consolidation enables wage suppression. Concession workers earn substantially less than equivalent franchise staff despite identical roles and skill demands. Collective bargaining fragments across independent vendor sites, weakening worker leverage and voice.

Recent labour campaigns identify vendor consolidation as a structural barrier to equitable working conditions in arenas. {Scholar, YYYY} documents how outsourcing to national vendors has suppressed worker protections and wage standards across stadium concessions. The vendor–revenue model, which concentrates profit margins with outside corporations and deflects labour accountability from franchises, remains largely unexamined in sports business scholarship.
