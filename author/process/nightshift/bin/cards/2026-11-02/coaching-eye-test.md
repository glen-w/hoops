# Coaching Eye Test

**Source key:** Baghurst et al., 2021; Ding et al., 2026; Demenius, undated; Scientific Basketball, undated

The coaching 'eye test'—subjective visual assessment of player ability, potential, and on-court fit—represents a traditional evaluation mode in NBA scouting and coaching, contrasting with the rise of advanced analytics and statistical frameworks. Baghurst et al. (2021) document how coaches 'rely on their perceptions to make athlete-related decisions, such as roster selection, playing time, and tactical strategies,' while cautioning that perceptions can conflict with reality due to attitudes, prior performances, and external conversations. Demenius (undated) notes that highest-level coaches often 'rely on their eye-test capabilities and intuition to make decisions during games,' though questioning whether this remains appropriate in the modern era.

Recent empirical work by Ding et al. (2026) operationalizes the eye test as a measurable cognitive process, using eye-tracking (Tobii Pro Glasses 2) to measure visual search patterns of elite and novice coaches analyzing game segments. The *Scientific Basketball* framework outlines subjective scouting observations (size, speed, aggressiveness, competitive ability, endurance, temperament, defensive ability) as foundational to coaching individual players and planning tactical strategies. Key research gaps: Do eye-test evaluations predict performance better than statistical proxies? How do coaching staffs integrate subjective judgment with analytics departments?
