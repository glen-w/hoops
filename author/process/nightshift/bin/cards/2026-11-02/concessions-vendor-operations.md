# Arena Concessions: Vendor Operations and Franchise Revenue

The professional sports arena food and beverage sector has been marked by vendor consolidation since the 1990s. Major vendors—including Aramark, Levy Restaurants, and Sports Service Enterprises (SSE)—operate under revenue-sharing agreements with franchises, managing labour, supply chains, and capital improvements to concourse infrastructure in exchange for a share of gross concession revenue.

This consolidation has created economies of scale in purchasing and labour deployment, but it has also centralised control over worker equity and menu curation. Premium seating food service—luxury suites, club sections, and premium concourses—generates higher-margin revenue than general concession stands, reflecting broader arena design trends toward segmented spectator experience. While general admission concession design prioritises throughput and speed (narrow concourses, limited vendor stations), luxury seating infrastructure enables bespoke catering, private dining, and premium merchandise bundling.

Labour conditions in arena food service remain fragmented: franchises either employ workers directly or delegate to vendors, who typically hire seasonal or part-time workers. Worker equity, benefits, and union representation vary widely. The relationship between vendor consolidation, labour conditions, and franchise profitability remains understudied.
