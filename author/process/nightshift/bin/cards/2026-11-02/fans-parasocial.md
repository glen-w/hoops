Parasocial relationships—one-sided emotional connections to media figures—represent a significant dimension of basketball fandom. Fans may develop intense attachments to players, coaches, or broadcasters despite lacking reciprocal interaction. In basketball, such bonds intensify through various vectors: frequent courtside visibility, player social media presence, autobiographical narratives, and the immediacy of live broadcast.

Research on sports fandom {Wann and James, 2018} demonstrates that parasocial attachment correlates with fan identity and self-concept integration. Fans incorporate player success into their own psychological well-being, internalising victories as personal triumphs. This psychological mechanism can be protective—fostering community and belonging—or pathological, manifesting as obsessive monitoring, aggression towards perceived threats to the player, or maladaptive coping during losses.

{Harasta, 2021} explores the darker dimensions of sports parasociality, including boundary violations when fans confuse mediated intimacy with genuine relationship. Digital platforms amplify parasocial risk: fan-generated content, hashtag campaigns, and direct messaging create illusions of reciprocity. Crucially, parasocial attachment intersects with fan identity formation and tribal belonging, complicating straightforward pathology assessments. Understanding parasocial dynamics requires attention to both psychological vulnerability and social scaffolding—the communities that channel attachment towards collective meaning-making rather than isolation.
