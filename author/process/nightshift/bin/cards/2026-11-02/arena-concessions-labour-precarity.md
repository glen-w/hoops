**Vendor Consolidation and Worker Precarity in Arena Food Operations**

The consolidation of NBA arena food services among major contractors (Aramark, Levy, SSE) shapes labour conditions at the point of production. When franchises outsource food operations to large vendors, workers lose franchise-direct protections and depend on vendor-level labour policies. Vendor consolidation amplifies this exposure: standardised (minimum-wage) labour schemes, limited benefit coverage, and wage risk shifting onto workers across multiple venues.

The precarity question is structural: does vendor consolidation stabilise labour by offering consistent, scaled operations, or does it deepen precarity by enabling wage pressure and benefit minimisation across a vendor's portfolio? Franchises that operate food services in-house retain labour control and can tie worker equity to franchise success; franchises that outsource treat labour as a vendor-managed cost centre, insulating franchise margins from labour outcomes.

Scholarly analysis of labour outcomes in sports food operations is minimal. Business analyses and franchise financial reports typically treat vendor contracts as back-office efficiency plays, not labour-equity drivers. The relationship between franchise outsourcing decisions, vendor labour standards, and worker precarity remains largely understudied, particularly as vendors consolidate market share.
