## Queer Representation in Professional Basketball

Professional men's basketball exhibits marked institutional resistance to queer visibility. Moscowitz et al. (2018) demonstrate that "there are currently no out athletes playing in the major American men's leagues"—a condition they attribute to the sport operating within a "sports closet" enforcing heterosexuality and hypermasculinity as hegemonic identity norms.

The WNBA presents a contrasting institutional culture. Kabetu (n.d.) characterises it as "a safe and affirming space for the queer community because there are many queer players in the league." Rallins (n.d.) explores how Black queer athletes leverage self-representation within the WNBA to resist misogynoir and heteronormativity, suggesting that institutional structure—not basketball culture itself—determines inclusion patterns.

Historically, Kian et al. (2015) document that "sport media coverage historically ignored gay athletes and issues." Pivotal exceptions—Jason Collins's NBA disclosure and Michael Sam's NFL announcement—marked rare ruptures in institutional silence. Pariera et al. (2019) reveal continuing "don't ask, don't tell" cultures at collegiate level, rooted in sports' foundational assumption of "universal heterosexuality" (Pronger, 1992). This gap between men's and women's professional structures warrants deeper investigation into how institutional rules, governance, and fan culture reproduce or permit queer inclusion.
