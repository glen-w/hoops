# Vendor Consolidation and Concession Worker Equity

Major food-service contractors—Aramark, Levy Restaurants, Sports Service Enterprises—have dominated NBA arena concessions since the early 1980s, creating standardised labour regimes and centralised pricing that affect thousands of seasonal concession workers. Franchise profitability increasingly intertwines with vendor consolidation and revenue-sharing, yet scholarship remains fragmented: business analyses document vendor contracts and revenue-sharing with franchises, whilst labour studies examine stadium workers' wage precarity, but few sources integrate both perspectives. Gramling's archival work on stadium labour dynamics offers a methodological foundation for grounding concession equity in historical wage data and worker testimony, yet comprehensive comparative study of vendor labour practices across NBA franchises is absent. The research gap is acute: how does vendor consolidation correlate with wage compression or labour precarity? Do revenue-sharing models between franchises and major contractors align with equitable wage floors for concession staff? Future scholarship should synthesise labour archives, vendor corporate disclosures, and franchise financial records to clarify the labour equity trade-offs embedded in modern arena food-service contracting.
