**Broadcast cameras and the visual framing of basketball**

The camera's position on court fundamentally shapes how spectators perceive the game. Multiple camera angles—baseline, sideline, overhead, extreme close-up—create a compositional language that highlights certain aspects whilst downplaying others. The fixed overhead view emphasises spacing and ball movement; tight sideline angles create illusion of defensive pressure.

Evolution from fixed tripod mounts to mobile remote-operated systems has expanded production flexibility, allowing real-time zoom on player reactions, free-throw concentration, or bench dynamics. Technical choices—frame rate (broadcast vs. slow motion), colour grading, audio sync—become editorial decisions that structure narrative emphasis. Early broadcast basketball relied on single static cameras; modern coverage deploys 15+ feeds simultaneously, allowing editors to construct meaning in real time.

Camera placement relative to the three-dimensional court creates systematic bias: high-angle views flatten spacing perception; close-ups intensify emotional register. No neutral observational stance exists in broadcast design.
