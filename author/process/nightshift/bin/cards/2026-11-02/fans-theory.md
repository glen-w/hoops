**Fans theory**

Basketball fandom constitutes a psychologically and sociologically rich domain for studying identity formation, community belonging, and spectatorship. Foundational research (Wann & James, 2018; Harasta, 2021) examines how fans engage with teams and players as extensions of self, experiencing affective, behavioral, and cognitive reactions that provide "a sense of belonging, of uniqueness, and of meaning in life." Fan identity develops through socialisation processes, whereby teams, players, and geographic attachments become integrated into fans' self-concept (Branscombe & Wann, 1991; Jacobson, 2003). Contemporary scholarship interrogates how individual psychology—attachment styles, sensation-seeking, identity salience—intersects with collective fan culture to shape emotional investment and community participation. Research addresses gender and race dimensions of fandom; digital fan communities and social-media-enabled connection; and the economic structures that enable fan participation. Sports offer identity dimensions "much more salient than other areas of individuals' personalities" (O'Leary, 2023), yet scholarship also investigates fandom's "darker side"—fan aggression, parasocial pathology, and psychological risks. Contemporary analysis distinguishes between casual spectatorship and intense fandom, exploring how teams cultivate loyalty through marketing and engagement whilst fans construct meaning through collective participation.
