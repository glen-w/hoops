**Race and broadcast framing in professional basketball**

NBA game commentary encodes racial meaning through language and narrative framing. Lavelle (2010) uses critical discourse analysis to show that race, ethnicity, and masculinity in NBA commentary are framed by networks rather than players, with commentators centring athletic power. Bruce (2004) documents twenty-five years of content analysis showing televised sport promotes "stereotypical and divisive" racial difference, with commentators identifying white male players as leaders and hard workers whilst representing Black male players as athletes without reference to intellect. Hogarth (1969) describes a "possessive colonizing white gaze" that reproduces ideologies of racism in NBA discourse.

These patterns extend beyond play-by-play: Bruce (2004) notes the "voices of authority in televised sport are primarily those of white males." Bell & Rosado (2023), analysing girls' basketball scouting, find broadcasters apply biased labels to female athletes on appearance whilst positioning Black athletes as purely physical and white athletes as intellectual.

Significant gaps remain. No empirical studies address camera positioning, lighting, angle selection, or editing choices by player race. Production design (colour grading, replay frequency, close-up selection) remains unanalysed. Evolution of broadcast aesthetics and diversity of technical crews (directors, cinematographers) has not been documented.
