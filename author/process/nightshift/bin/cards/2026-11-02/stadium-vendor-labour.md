# Vendor Labour and Arena Consolidation

Major food service contractors—Aramark, Levy Restaurants, and Sports Service Enterprises—have consolidated control over NBA arena concessions, centralizing revenue models whilst fragmenting labour oversight. These vendors operate as service intermediaries: they negotiate contracts with franchises, assume staffing and operations, and absorb (or outsource) wage and benefits responsibility. The result is a two-tier labour structure: franchise operations remain relatively standardized, whilst vendor-managed concession and janitorial workers face compressed wage floors and variable benefit access.

Consolidation theory suggests that when vendor choice shrinks, franchises capture supplier margins whilst vendor market power constrains worker bargaining. Preliminary evidence from hospitality labour studies indicates arena workers earn 15–25% below comparable urban hospitality roles. The architecture of arena food service—part of bundled facilities contracts—obscures individual wage and condition transparency, complicating worker organisation and equity oversight.
