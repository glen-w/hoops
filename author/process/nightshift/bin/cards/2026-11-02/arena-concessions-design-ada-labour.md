## Arena Concessions: Design, Accessibility, and Labour Integration

Contemporary NBA venues operate food concessions at the intersection of architectural design, accessibility compliance, labour conditions, and pricing equity. Physical infrastructure—concourse layouts, counter heights, ventilation, queue management—shapes both operational efficiency and back-of-house working conditions.

NBA arenas built or renovated since 2010 increasingly incorporate ADA accessibility requirements into concession design. Yet scholarly literature rarely examines how architectural mandates affect labour conditions. Wheelchair-accessible counters require reconfigured kitchen workflows; service standards pitched across seating tiers—nosebleed to luxury suites—create tiered labour demands. Major vendors (Aramark, Levy Restaurants) standardise protocols across multiple venues, yet arena-specific design generates friction in worker scheduling and safety.

Pricing compounds accessibility challenges. Concession markups reflect vendor margins and venue rental costs passed to fans. Premium seating subsidises general concourse pricing, but data on how pricing correlates with attendance equity remain sparse.

The scholarly gap is methodological: research isolates food security, labour standards, or architectural accessibility; few integrate all three. Sports business literature privileges franchise revenue; labour studies exclude stadium hospitality. Bridging this requires interdisciplinary work grounding vendor contracts, worker testimony, and design documentation.
