# NBA Arena Design and Seating Capacity Evolution (1950–2000)

Milwaukee Arena (1950) marked the first multipurpose arena in the modern sports era, seating 11,046 and costing $6 million—establishing a template for expansion-era construction (Downs & Seifried, 2023). Public financing for sports venues accelerated in the 1950s–1960s as early-1900s facilities showed excessive wear; league expansion made rehabilitation of older, limited-capacity venues economically unviable (Propheter, 2012). Seating capacity expanded dramatically: Key Arena in Seattle (1967) accommodated 24,022 for basketball, reflecting growing commercial demands. By the 1980s, newly constructed public arenas for basketball outpaced other sports, driven by expansion into Charlotte, Miami, Minnesota, and Orlando (Propheter, 2012). Expansion-era venues introduced state-of-the-art building codes and architectural standards governing all subsequent construction and renovation (Downs & Seifried, 2023). Scholarly analyses to date have focused on political-economic factors driving arena construction while neglecting consumer perspectives and capitalist ambitions in venue design (Downs & Seifried, 2023). This gap suggests an opportunity to examine how franchise profitability objectives shaped capacity and architectural choices across the expansion era.
