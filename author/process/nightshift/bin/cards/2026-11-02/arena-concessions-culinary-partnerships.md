Contemporary NBA arenas commodify culinary identity. Franchises court celebrity chefs, curate local sourcing, and script menu narratives—yet culinary programming remains absent from franchise scholarship. Food studies orbits labour equity and concession standardisation; it does not examine how arenas weaponise food experience as brand strategy. The Barclays Center sources Brooklyn suppliers; NOLA's Smoothie King Center partners regional chefs; yet who documents whether these partnerships genuinely engage community or perform it? Levy Restaurants and Aramark broker vast concession contracts; yet no business scholarship traces how vendor consolidation constrains local culinary partnership. The gap is strategic: franchises extract cultural labour from regional chefs and local producers—naming, curation, aesthetic integration—yet pay neither scholars nor standards to study the exchange.
