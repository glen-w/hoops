Professional sports arenas embed labour within capitalism's hierarchy. Chen (2022) sketches sport's class "pyramid"—transnational elites at the apex, a partial-control middle tier, and a broad working-class base—yet sport-management scholarship has largely avoided naming capitalism's structural role. Arena food and beverage operations exemplify this neglect: concession workers, vendor staff, catering crews form an invisible labour stratum.

Rosen & Sanderson analyse collective bargaining (reverse-order drafts, payroll caps, revenue sharing) as rent-redistribution mechanisms between players and owners; such structures leave concession labour unshielded. Downs & Seifried position arenas as "intentional products of capitalism" centred on pricing systems, property rights, and revenue extraction—yet do not isolate concession vendor selection and wage architectures as deliberate tools in that extraction.
