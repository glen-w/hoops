# NBA Broadcast Audience Measurement Systems, 1960–2000

Broadcast audience metrics determined professional basketball sponsorship valuations from 1960 to 2000. Nielsen ratings systems, the primary industry measurement standard, quantified television viewership using diary panels (early period) and electronic metering (later period). Sponsorship fees—the primary broadcast revenue source—were negotiated directly as functions of reported audience size. Discrepancies between measurement methodologies and actual viewership created technical and financial tensions within broadcast negotiations; the transition from diary-based to electronic systems introduced new measurement assumptions. Franchise revenue forecasts depended critically on audience metrics, making rating accuracy financially consequential for franchise competitive positioning. The documented evolution of measurement systems and industry responses to methodological change illuminate how audience metrics functioned as financial instruments within professional basketball's broadcast economics, fundamentally shaping franchise competitive capacity across markets. Sponsorship disputes about rating accuracy reveal the financial stakes of measurement methodology in professional basketball.
