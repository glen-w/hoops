# Arena Operations and Market Viability in the NBA, 1950–2000

NBA arena economics drove franchise revenue and competitive capacity across five decades. Early venues (Boston Garden, Madison Square Garden III, Kiel Auditorium) were multi-purpose halls with limited seating (9,000–18,000), minimal luxury inventory, and lease terms favouring building owners. Gate receipts dominated revenue (60–80% pre-1980). The 1960s–1970s saw suburban arena construction (The Forum, Spectrum, MECCA) with larger capacities (15,000–17,000) but poor sightlines and no premium seating. Revenue shifted slowly: sponsorship signage (1970s), club seats (1980s), naming rights (1990s). Relocation decisions tracked market demographics — Milwaukee to St. Louis (1955), Cincinnati to Kansas City (1972), San Diego to Houston (1971), New Orleans to Utah (1979), Kansas City to Sacramento (1985), Vancouver to Memphis (2001). Local market size (MSA population, corporate base) correlated with franchise valuation and payroll capacity. The 1990s "new arena boom" (20+ facilities) introduced luxury suites (50–150 per venue), premium clubs, and revenue-sharing lease structures that decoupled team finances from gate dependence.
