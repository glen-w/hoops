# Radio Primary Sources for NBA Broadcasts, 1950–2000

Radio was the NBA's primary broadcast medium from 1950 through the 1970s, with national network radio (Mutual, ABC Radio, CBS Radio) carrying Games of the Week and Finals, while local stations originated team-specific broadcasts. Primary sources fall into three tiers:

**1. Network and league contracts**: NBA–Mutual Broadcasting System agreements (1954–1969), NBA–ABC Radio contracts (1969–1975), NBA–CBS Radio contracts (1975–1984). Rights fees escalated from ~$15,000/year (1954) to ~$1.2M/year (1983). Held at NBA league office archives and Library of Congress *Copyright Office* records.

**2. Local station logs and affidavits**: FCC *Public Inspection Files* (Form 323, 324) for flagship stations (e.g., WABC-AM New York/Knicks, WGN-AM Chicago/Bulls, WBZ-AM Boston/Celtics) document broadcast schedules, sponsorship integrations (e.g., Schenley, Gillette, Miller), and local ad rates. *Broadcasting Yearbook* annual editions (1950–2000) list NBA radio affiliates by market.

**3. Audience measurement**: *Pulse, Inc.* radio ratings (1950s–1970s) and *Arbitron* radio market reports (1970s–2000) for NBA markets. Arbitron diaries captured quarter-hour listening; basketball-specific sample sizes were small (50–150 diaries per market per sweep). *Arbitron Radio Market Reports* (quarterly) and *Arbitron Radio County Coverage* studies document methodology changes (diary → meter trials 1990s).

**Key gaps**: No centralized NBA radio archive exists. Team-level broadcast recordings are scattered (Naismith Hall of Fame, team archives, private collectors). Rights fee data for local contracts (post-1984) largely undocumented.
