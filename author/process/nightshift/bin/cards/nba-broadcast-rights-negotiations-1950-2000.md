Professional basketball broadcast rights negotiations from 1950 to 2000 evolved from local station deals to national network packages driving franchise revenue growth. Early period (1950s–1960s): NBA sold rights piecemeal to regional stations (DuMont, local independents); fees negligible — 1954 NBC deal $39,000/year for playoff games. Competitive bidding emerged 1970s: CBS (1973–1990) paid escalating fees ($27M/yr by 1989) but NBA retained playoff control. 1990 NBC deal ($600M/4yr) marked first true auction — CBS, ABC, NBC, Fox bid; NBC won with revenue-sharing clause tying fees to ratings guarantees. 2002 ABC/ESPN/TNT deal ($4.6B/6yr) introduced cable competition (TNT) and dual-revenue streams (rights fees + advertising). Key drivers: cable penetration (ESPN launch 1979), demographic targeting (18–49), playoff inventory scarcity. Network strategies: CBS used NBA to anchor weekend sports; NBC leveraged Jordan-era ratings; TNT bought playoff exclusivity for cable credibility. Franchise impact: national rights revenue share grew from <5% of team revenue (1970) to ~30% (2000). Primary sources: NBA Broadcast Committee minutes (1970–2000), Sports Business Journal archives, network press releases, Senate Commerce Committee hearings (1992, 1998), Broadcasting & Cable.
