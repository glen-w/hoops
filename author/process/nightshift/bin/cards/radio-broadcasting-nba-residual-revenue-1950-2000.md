# Radio Broadcasting as a Residual Revenue Category in NBA Finance, 1950–2000

**Jozsa, 2018; Hausman & Leonard, 1997**

Radio broadcasting appears in the scholarly literature on professional basketball finance as a named but undifferentiated revenue stream. Jozsa (2018) lists "television, radio and Internet broadcasts" alongside gate receipts, concessions, and merchandise as principal franchise revenue sources, but isolates neither fees nor audience data for radio. More significantly, Hausman & Leonard (1997) report that "the contribution of 'media' (television and radio) to total league revenue increased from 29% during the 1980s"—the only quantified estimate found—yet does not disaggregate radio's proportion or fee structure.

This pattern reveals a scholarly gap: radio rights, audience, and sponsorship metrics for the 1950–2000 period remain absent from the examined business and economic literature on basketball. Radio appears only as a residual category bundled with television, obscuring its actual historical contribution to franchise revenue and fan engagement. The absence of detailed scholarly treatment suggests radio's financial role either declined sharply with television's rise or remains an undocumented element of franchise income during this half-century. Archive research and primary documents (industry records, franchise ledgers, trade publications) may be required to quantify radio's independent economic value.
