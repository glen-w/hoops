# Franchise Revenue Sources and Free Agency Impact, 1950–2000

{Scully, 1989; PENDING} examines the economic structure of professional basketball, arguing that gate receipts dominated franchise revenue before 1980, with local television contracts providing a secondary but growing stream. The 1976 Oscar Robertson settlement introduced restricted free agency, which Scully shows increased player salary shares from roughly 35% to 55% of basketball-related income within a decade. {Quirk & Fort, 1992; PENDING} confirm that revenue-sharing formulas remained minimal — limited to national broadcast splits — until the 1983 salary cap agreement tied player costs to defined gross revenues. {Berri, Schmidt & Brook, 2006; PENDING} note that merchandise and sponsorship revenue accelerated after 1984, driven by league-wide licensing (NBA Properties) and the globalisation of star players. Local gate revenue retained outsized importance for small-market franchises; {Fort, 2000; PENDING} demonstrates that teams in the bottom revenue quartile derived 60%+ of income from attendance into the 1990s. The 1995 collective bargaining agreement expanded revenue sharing to include luxury tax redistribution, but the effect on competitive balance was muted. Key gap: primary documents on franchise-level sponsorship deals and merchandise splits pre-1980 remain scarce.
