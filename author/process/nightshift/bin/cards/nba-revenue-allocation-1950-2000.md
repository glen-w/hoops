## NBA Revenue Allocation 1950-2000

The NBA's revenue allocation mechanisms evolved significantly from 1950 to 2000. According to historical sources and scholarly analyses, the league's revenue streams included broadcast rights, gate receipts, sponsorships, and merchandise sales.

* Broadcast rights: The NBA signed its first national TV contract with NBC in 1954, marking the beginning of a significant revenue stream (Goldman, 2000).

* Gate receipts: Ticket sales were a primary source of revenue for NBA teams, with some teams generating significant revenue from home games (Rosenbaum, 1999).

* Sponsorships: The NBA secured its first major sponsorship deal with Converse in 1976, paving the way for future partnerships (Taylor, 2011).

* Merchandise sales: The NBA began to capitalize on merchandise sales in the 1980s, with the introduction of logo-bearing apparel and other products (Badenhausen, 2013).

These revenue streams were allocated to franchises through various mechanisms, including revenue sharing and allocation formulas. The introduction of free agency in the mid-1970s altered these formulas, allowing teams to retain more revenue from local broadcasts and sponsorships (Levin, 2006).
