# NBA Arena Bond Financing and Public-Private Models, 2000–2026

Modern professional sports arena development relies on municipal bond issuance to bridge franchise capital needs and public venue investment. The Little Caesars Arena (Detroit, 2017) was financed through Detroit's Development Authority, which issued senior TI refunding bonds with a par value of $287.4 million. The Amway Center in Orlando (2010, later rebranded Kia Center) employed senior refunding revenue bonds based on sixth-cent contract payments, with a par value of $159.9 million. These structures create asymmetric risk: municipalities absorb capital cost inflation while franchises control operating revenue and naming rights.

Construction cost inflation has dramatically reshaped arena economics. The Engineering News-Record Construction Cost Index (20-city aggregate) rose from 6,221 in 2000 to 13,843 in 2026, representing a 122% real increase. This inflation pressure accelerates venue obsolescence cycles and incentivizes earlier bond refinancing. Modern arena disclosure documents increasingly subordinate public interests (community access, affordability) to debt service priorities and franchise profit extraction, making public financing essential for franchise competitiveness in mid-market cities.
