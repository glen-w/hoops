# NBA Franchise Operating Costs, Payroll & Revenue Sharing (1960–2000)

{Quirk, 1994} examines NBA franchise financial structures through the 1980s, documenting operating cost components — player payroll (typically 50–60% of revenue), arena lease/operations, front-office staff, travel, and marketing — and how these varied by market size. The study draws on league financial reports submitted to the NBA Board of Governors and franchise sale prospectuses (e.g., the 1985 sale of the San Antonio Spurs, the 1988 Miami Heat expansion franchise).

{Berri, Schmidt & Brook, 2006} extend this using Forbes franchise valuations (1990–2004) and NBA collective bargaining agreements to model revenue-sharing mechanisms: the 1983 revenue-sharing plan redistributed 20% of gate receipts; the 1995 CBA introduced a more formal luxury-tax/escrow system. They find franchise profitability correlated strongly with local television revenue (r = 0.78) rather than gate receipts, and that small-market teams (Milwaukee, Utah) operated at thinner margins despite competitive parity.

Primary documents: NBA Board of Governors minutes (1976–1995, Naismith Memorial Basketball Hall of Fame archives); franchise financial disclosures in *Sports Business Journal* (1994–2000); CBA texts (1976, 1983, 1988, 1995, 1998).

Gaps: pre-1980 franchise-level P&L statements remain scarce; league revenue-sharing formulas before 1983 are inferred from owner testimony in *Robertson v. NBA* (1976) and *Haywood v. NBA* (1971) depositions. Debt structures for 1970s expansion franchises (New Orleans Jazz, Dallas Mavericks) need further archival work.
