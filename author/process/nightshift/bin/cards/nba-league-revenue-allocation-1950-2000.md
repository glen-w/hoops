# NBA League Revenue Allocation and Television Rights (1950–2000)

From 1950 to 2000, television rights became the dominant revenue stream structuring NBA league operations and franchise distribution systems. Neuhaus & Thomas (2024) trace the escalation from "unsustainable levels" in the 1970s to US$100 million for 1982–85, then to US$4.6 billion covering 1989–2002, reflecting intense competition for sports broadcasting rights. Fort (2024) demonstrates how rising national television revenues offset financial losses during the transition from reserve clauses to free agency (1976–1993), enabling cross-subsidisation of lower-drawing franchises.

International television expansion amplified revenue reach: Zhang (2023) documents NBA International's regional offices in Geneva, Barcelona, Melbourne, Hong Kong, Mexico City, and Miami negotiating contracts with national broadcasters and satellite operators. By 1995, the Finals reached 160+ countries and regions. The 2004 legal analysis of NBA luxury tax models reveals television rights fees as fixed, contractually defined inputs in the league's "Projected BRI" calculation, whilst Hausman & Leonard (1997) identify national television alongside gate receipts, sponsorships, and merchandise as the four largest revenue sources.
