# Contemporary Arena Refinancing: Public Bonds and Venue Capital (2017–2018)

NBA arenas operate within public-private financing structures where municipal bonds backed by dedicated revenue streams enable ongoing capital investment. Two contemporary case studies illustrate these mechanisms.

**Little Caesars Arena (Detroit):** The city issued 2018A senior TI refunding bonds with $287.4 million in principal (DDA of Detroit, Official Statement, 2019). Senior TI designations indicate priority claim on venue revenues, allowing the municipality to refinance earlier debt while funding facility upgrades.

**Amway Center/Kia Center (Orlando):** The 2017A senior TDT refunding revenue bonds ($159.9 million outstanding as of 30 September 2024) similarly refinanced prior obligations through revenue structures dedicated to the venue's cash flows (City of Orlando, Bond Disclosure Documents, 2024).

Both structures reflect how public capital markets finance arena modernization. The bonds are backed by dedicated revenue streams—typically arena rent, ticket surcharges, or facility rental income. The ENR Construction Cost Index (2000–2026) demonstrates the real cost escalation driving these refinancing cycles. These mechanisms shape venue competitiveness, determining which franchises can invest in state-of-the-art facilities and attract major events.

**Sources:** City of Detroit DDA, 2018A Official Statement (2019); City of Orlando Bond Disclosure (2024); ENR CCI Annual Data (2000–2026).
