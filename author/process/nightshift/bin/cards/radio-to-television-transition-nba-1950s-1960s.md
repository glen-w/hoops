# Radio-to-Television Transition in NBA Broadcasting (1950s–1960s)

NBA radio play-by-play (early 1950s) built regional fan bases through local announcers and sponsors. As television adoption grew post-1955, broadcast licensing split between team-owned regional networks and league-licensed national contracts. Early commentators—Johnny Most (Boston), Chick Hearn (Los Angeles)—created distinctive local followings. Regional syndication disparity meant major metro markets received more broadcast hours than secondary cities, shaping franchise fan development and long-term viability. The shift from radio to television (1960s) reflected competing strategies: maximize team revenue via local control, or grow league visibility via national reach.
