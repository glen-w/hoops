# Tax-Base Assumptions in Arena Refunding: Detroit and Orlando, 2015–2018

Municipal arena financing in the 2010s relied on tax-increment (TI) and tourism development tax (TDT) mechanisms, both structured around explicit assumptions about future tax-base growth. Detroit's Little Caesars Arena (2018A refunding) and Orlando's Amway Center (2017A refunding) illustrate how prospectuses anchored debt repayment to local economic growth projections.

Bond documents embedded baseline tax-base growth rates and deflation covenants. The 2017–2018 refundings explicitly documented assumptions about sales-tax revenue, property-value appreciation, and visitor-spending trajectories. Grokbot extraction of primary prospectuses (City of Detroit and City of Orlando disclosure documents) recovered these assumptions alongside contemporary inflation indices (ENR CCI, 2000–2026).

Comparative analysis reveals asymmetry: Detroit's TI mechanism subordinated arena debt to other public obligations; Orlando's TDT directly pledged visitor tax. Both reserved covenant-adjustment clauses tied to actual vs. forecast tax-base performance. Understanding these assumptions is essential for evaluating whether arena financing created franchise moral hazard or public fiscal discipline.
