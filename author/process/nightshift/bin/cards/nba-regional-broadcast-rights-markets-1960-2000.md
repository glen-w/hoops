# Regional Broadcast Rights and Local Revenue Markets, 1960–2000

The NBA adopted a hybrid broadcast structure wherein the league's central office controlled national contracts whilst franchises governed local broadcasts. From 1980, the Board of Governors capped each team at 41 over-the-air free-broadcast games—half the regular season—preserving scarcity and commercial value (Bell, n.d.; N4V2QEU3).

During 1990–91, NBC carried 22 games and TNT 50 nationally, whilst regional sports networks served local markets through upper-tier cable requiring subscriber fees (Hausman & Leonard, 1997; 5ZFB9FVM). This structure enabled franchises to capture local revenue whilst the league preserved national broadcast control.

Regional distributors and broadcasters negotiated through interdependent networks; international expansion demonstrated how national networks and regional satellite providers (Star, ESPN International, Sky Sport) supplemented league distribution (Zhang, 2023; E4APG5PE). The hybrid model remained stable from 1960–2000, balancing centralised national rights revenue with localised market capture.
