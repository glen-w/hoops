# Arena Revenue Bonds: Nominal Debt Scale and Refunding Cycles

Little Caesars Arena (Detroit) carried $287.4M in 2018A senior tax-increment refunding bonds, while Amway Center (Orlando) held $159.9M in 2017A senior tourism-development-tax refunding revenue bonds. Refunding—issuing new bonds to retire prior debt—reveals reliance on continuous capital markets access and rising nominal debt loads. When arenas deploy revenue bonds backed by specific tax streams (TIF/TDT), they subordinate future discretionary spending to debt service, shifting financial risk from private operators to public treasuries. The ENR Construction Cost Index shows cumulative 2000–2026 inflation of 27%, meaning a $100M 1990s arena construction now costs ~$127M—a gap often financed through successive refunding rather than new tax authority, deferring burden to later-year taxpayers. This refunding pattern suggests franchise profitability has decoupled from arena capital costs, cementing public subsidy as structural rather than transitional.
