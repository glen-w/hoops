## NBA Arena Public Funding Models, 2010–2026

Contemporary NBA arena construction and renovation relies on public-private partnerships and municipal bond financing. Little Caesars Arena (Detroit, 2017) completed a $287.4M senior TI refunding bond offering for venue TI and renovation (Grokbot, 2026). Amway Center (Orlando, 2017) issued $159.9M in senior TDT refunding revenue bonds tied to sixth-cent contract payments (Grokbot, 2026). These structures emerge from tax increment financing (TIF) districts and facility revenue bonds tied to local sales/tourism taxes—placing arena viability within broader municipal fiscal health.

Construction cost inflation, indexed by ENR CCI (20-city composite), rose from 6,221 (2000) to 9,721+ (2026), a 56% increase (Grokbot, 2026). Franchises and municipalities balance arena modernization pressure (video boards, climate, seating) against revenue caps and salary-cap constraints. Public bond offerings shift risk from franchises to municipal taxpayers; yet arena revenue sharing (parking, concessions, premium seating) feeds franchise operating budgets and competitive flexibility.
