# Broadcast ratings and sponsor valuation (1960–2000)

**PENDING** (synthesised from broadcast finance and sponsorship literature)

Broadcast ratings—measured through audience surveys and Nielsen-style methodologies—became a primary financial lever for NBA franchise economics from the 1960s onward. As television revenue grew from sponsorship and advertising, the measurement systems themselves shaped franchise valuation. Sponsors based advertising spend and premium rates on documented reach and household penetration. However, tensions emerged between claimed viewership (reported ratings) and actual viewership; disputes over rating accuracy directly influenced sponsorship fees and broadcast rights negotiations. A franchise or network claiming higher ratings could command higher sponsorship rates, whilst discrepancies between measurement systems or rating revisions could trigger renegotiation. This created a financial incentive to ensure measurement credibility—or to dispute unfavourable results. The relationship was direct: ratings → advertiser value → sponsor fees → franchise revenue. Understanding broadcast financial architecture therefore requires examining both the technology of audience measurement (Nielsen, local metres, surveys) and the financial stakes sponsors and franchises attached to rating claims.
