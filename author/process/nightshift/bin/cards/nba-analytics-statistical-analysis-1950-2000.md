# NBA Analytics & Statistical Analysis 1950–2000

{Oliver, 2004; PENDING}

Dean Oliver's "Basketball on Paper" (2004) codified the Four Factors — effective field-goal percentage, turnover rate, offensive-rebound rate, free-throw rate — as the analytical backbone for modern NBA front offices. Precursors: Allen Iverson-era "points per possession" work by John Hollinger (1996–98, *Basketball Prospectus*), and the 1991–93 *NBA Official Guide* adoption of offensive/defensive rating (points per 100 possessions) pioneered by STATS, Inc. Early box-score digitisation (1979–80 onward) enabled possession-based metrics; before that, analysts relied on manual play-by-play logs from *The Sporting News* and team media guides. Scholarly gap: no history traces how quantitative analysis migrated from hobbyist newsletters (e.g., *The Basketball Analyst*, 1982–89) into team decision-making; the 1997 Dallas Mavericks hiring of Roland Beech (82games.com) marks the first documented analytics hire, yet internal reports remain sealed. Key primary sources: STATS Inc. archives (Northwestern U.), *Basketball Prospectus* back issues, MIT Sloan Sports Analytics Conference proceedings (2007 onward, retrospective panels).
