# NBA Revenue Sharing 1950–2000

Early NBA revenue sharing was minimal. The 1950s–1960s league relied on gate receipts; teams kept local revenue. National TV contracts (1954 DuMont, 1964 ABC) paid modest fees split equally but amounted to <10% of income (Surdam, 2013; PENDING).

The 1976 ABA merger and 1977 Oscar Robertson settlement introduced player free agency, pressuring small-market franchises. The 1983 salary cap (first in pro sports) included a revenue-sharing pool: 53% of defined gross revenue (DGR) to players, with a luxury tax redistributing excess payroll (Coon, 2011; PENDING).

1990s expansion (Orlando, Minnesota, Toronto, Vancouver) brought expansion fees shared among existing owners. The 1995 CBA increased revenue sharing: national TV split equally; 40% of gate receipts to visiting team (later reduced). The 1998 lockout produced the 1999 CBA: escrow system, maximum salaries, and a more robust revenue-sharing plan targeting competitive balance (Fort & Quirk, 1995; PENDING).

Scholarly consensus: NBA revenue sharing remained weaker than NFL until 2011 CBA. Pre-2000 mechanisms were ad hoc, driven by labour disputes not proactive balance (Berri et al., 2009; PENDING).

Key gaps: franchise-level audited financials 1950–1980; regional TV revenue splits; merchandise/licensing pool mechanics pre-1990.
