# Broadcast Journalism & NBA Coverage, 1950–2000

{Broadcast Journalism NBA, 2024; PENDING}

Sports broadcast journalism evolved from newspaper-dominated reporting to multi-platform television and radio ecosystems. Early NBA coverage relied on local sportswriters — often team-affiliated — with limited editorial independence. Network television (CBS, NBC) introduced national commentators like Chick Hearn and Marv Albert, whose play-by-play styles shaped public perception. The 1980s cable boom (ESPN, TBS) created analyst roles for former players, blending expertise with entertainment. Team-owned regional networks (e.g., MSG, Prism) blurred journalistic lines, while league-controlled feeds (NBA Entertainment) standardised highlights. Sponsor influence (e.g., Gillette, Coca-Cola) affected segment placement. Primary sources: *Sporting News* archives, *Television/Radio Age*, network broadcast logs, oral histories (Naismith Hall of Fame), FCC filings. Scholarly: Crawford (2016) on media–league symbiosis; Vogan (2018) on ESPN's institutional power; McChesney (1989) on commercial pressures. Gaps: radio commentator contracts, local vs. national editorial tension, 1990s digital transition.
