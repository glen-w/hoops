## Key Themes & Gaps

**1. Regional Network Development (1960s–1980s)**
- Early NBA relied on syndicated regional packages: e.g., *NBA on Hughes Television Network* (1970s), *Mizlou Television Network* (1970s–80s).
- Team-owned/regional broadcasters: WGN (Bulls), WPIX (Knicks), PRISM (76ers), SportsChannel regional nets (1980s).
- **Gap**: No comprehensive scholarly map of which franchises controlled local rights vs. league-controlled packages by season.

**2. Broadcast Rights Structures & Revenue Sharing**
- Pre-1980s: Local rights negotiated individually; national rights (ABC, CBS) separate. League office took minimal cut.
- 1980s shift: NBA began pooling *some* national revenue; local rights remained franchise property — driving revenue disparity (Lakers/Knicks/Celtics vs. small markets).
- **Gap**: Year-by-year revenue splits (local vs. national) per franchise unavailable in published literature.

**3. Regional vs. National Revenue Tension**
- Superstation era (WTBS, WGN, WOR) expanded national reach but undermined local exclusivity.
- 1990s: Regional Sports Networks (RSNs) — Fox Sports Net, Comcast SportsNet — locked in long-term local deals; franchise valuations tied to RSN equity stakes (e.g., Knicks/MSG, Lakers/Prime Ticket).
- **Gap**: Contract terms, rights fees, and RSN equity structures largely proprietary.

**4. Measurement & Valuation**
- Nielsen local market ratings (diary → people-meter) determined ad rates; small-market franchises argued measurement gaps depressed revenue.
- **Gap**: NBA-specific Nielsen local market reports (1960–2000) not publicly archived.
