# Regional and Local Sports Television and Broadcasting Rights, 1960–2000

Professional basketball's broadcast landscape from 1960 to 2000 was shaped by both national television contracts and regional and local broadcasting rights. Local market arrangements created distinct revenue streams and defined the geographic reach and profitability of franchises across the country.

From the 1960s onwards, franchises sought to reach their home markets directly through local television contracts. Major metropolitan areas—New York, Los Angeles, Chicago, Boston—developed robust local broadcasting arrangements with affiliated television stations. However, regional variations in market size, station availability, and commercial viability meant that local broadcast revenues differed substantially across franchises, creating early competitive imbalances.

The emergence of cable television in the 1980s and dedicated regional sports networks (RSNs) fundamentally altered local broadcasting economics. RSNs such as SportsChannel and Fox Sports Network affiliates expanded capacity for regional basketball coverage, allowing franchises to generate incremental revenue through localised programming and extended game broadcasts, whilst cultivating regional fan bases and extending broadcast reach beyond traditional affiliated stations.

These local and regional structures generated significant financial disparities amongst franchises. Teams in large, densely populated markets commanded substantially higher local broadcast fees than teams in smaller markets, creating competitive and financial imbalances that persisted even as league-wide revenue sharing mechanisms developed.
