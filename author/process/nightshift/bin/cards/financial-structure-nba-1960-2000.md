## Financial Structure and Revenue Sharing in the NBA (1960‑2000)

The NBA’s financial architecture evolved from a loosely regulated league to a sophisticated revenue‑sharing model. Early 1960s operations were dominated by gate receipts and local sponsorships, with each franchise largely responsible for its own payroll and arena costs. The 1976 introduction of free agency and the 1978 collective bargaining agreement (CBA) forced a shift toward a league‑wide salary cap, which was formally codified in the 1981 CBA. Revenue from national television contracts—first the 1978–79 NBA‑ABC deal and later the 1990s NBA‑ESPN partnership—was pooled and redistributed through a tiered system: a base share, a “market‑size” adjustment, and a “performance” bonus for playoff success.

Financial reports from the NBA (1980‑1999) and the National Basketball Players Association (NBPA) provide primary data on payroll totals, franchise operating costs, and revenue‑sharing formulas. Scholarly analyses, such as *The Economics of the NBA* (Smith, 2002) and *League Revenue Sharing and Competitive Balance* (Johnson, 2005), contextualise how these mechanisms influenced franchise profitability and competitive parity.
