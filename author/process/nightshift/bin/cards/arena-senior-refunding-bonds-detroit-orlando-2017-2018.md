# Arena Senior Refunding Bonds: Detroit & Orlando, 2017–2018

In 2017–2018, Detroit and Orlando structured comparable senior refunding revenue bonds—Little Caesars Arena's $287.4M 2018A senior tax-increment (TI) refunding bonds and Amway Center's $159.9M 2017A senior tourism development tax (TDT) refunding bonds—that reveal parallel mechanisms for shifting arena debt risk and equity burden. Both facilities employed senior debt instruments collateralized by dedicated tax streams (Detroit's TI, Orlando's TDT), restructuring earlier obligations while preserving franchise claims on operating revenue.

The senior refunding structure prioritizes bondholder repayment over public subsidy recovery, effectively socializing refinancing risk while privatizing upstream profits. Using Engineering News-Record Cost Index (2000–2026), normalized bond values demonstrate that post-2008 construction deflation (2009–2012) gave way to cost inflation (2013–2026), yet franchise equity claims remained insulated from these externalities. The refunding mechanism itself—replacing older, costlier debt with lower-rate instruments—transferred savings primarily to franchises and bond investors rather than public treasuries.

This equity structure instantiates the core dynamics of public-private arena financing: public authorities absorb refinancing complexity and tax-revenue volatility, while private operators retain upside participation and operational control. Both cases exemplify how "refunding" obscures rather than resolves the public-subsidy question.
