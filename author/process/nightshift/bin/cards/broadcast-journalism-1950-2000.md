Early NBA coverage relied on newspaper sportswriters and local radio announcers who often doubled as team employees. Marty Glickman (Knicks radio) and Chick Hearn (Lakers radio/TV) pioneered play-by-play vocabulary still used today. Network television arrived sporadically: NBC's *Game of the Week* (1954–1962) and ABC's *Wide World of Sports* packages offered national exposure but limited editorial control.

The 1970s–1980s saw tension between team-owned broadcasters (e.g., Celtics' Johnny Most) and network crews. Sponsors (Gillette, Schick) bought halftime segments, influencing story selection. League-controlled feeds emerged with NBA Entertainment (1982), standardising highlights and narratives. Cable (ESPN 1982, TNT 1989) introduced studio analysts — former players/coaches — shifting authority from journalists to insiders.

Key gaps: quantitative studies of sponsor influence on copy; comparative analysis of team vs. network broadcaster independence; archival access to NBA Entertainment editorial guidelines.
