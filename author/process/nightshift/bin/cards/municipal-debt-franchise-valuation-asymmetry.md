## Municipal Debt Service and Private Franchise Valuation: Asymmetric Risk in Public Arena Financing

Detroit's Little Caesars Arena (2017) and Orlando's Amway Center exemplify asymmetric public-private risk in post-2010 NBA arena financing. Municipal authorities issued $287.4M and $159.9M in senior revenue bonds with fixed-rate coupons and 20–30-year maturities, secured by dedicated revenue streams (Detroit's TI, Orlando's TDT).

Fixed nominal debt obligations expose municipalities to inflation risk. The ENR Construction Cost Index rose 45% from 2000–2024. Arena operating costs track inflation, yet municipal debt service remains nominally fixed. Municipalities absorb long-term fiscal drag as real revenues erode against nominally fixed payments.

Franchise balance sheets capture appreciation upside. As NBA team valuations rise (driven by media-rights growth and expanding local revenues), franchise owners' equity appreciates. Municipalities service fixed debt against inflation-eroded tax bases. The franchise captures revenue growth and market appreciation; the municipality absorbs inflation risk and debt-service burden.

This asymmetry characterizes post-2010 arena deals: franchises secured modern facilities at favorable terms while communities absorbed decades of fiscal exposure.
