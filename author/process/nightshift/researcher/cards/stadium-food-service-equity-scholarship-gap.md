The scholarly record on NBA arena food service—particularly how dietary diversity, menu innovation, and pricing stratify across seating tiers—remains sparse. Historical work (Downs & Seifried) documents tiered consumption in venues from the 1950s–1980s, establishing that arena design has long choreographed access along wealth lines through private suites and distinct concession stands. Contemporary league reports note food-recovery initiatives and vendor-diversity programmes, but these address only margins of the larger question.

No extant scholarship directly examines how post-2010 NBA arenas have implemented dietary accommodations (vegan, allergen-conscious), curated menus via regional-chef partnerships, or managed pricing equity across concourse tiers. The gap is telling: food service sits at the intersection of operational margin, cultural representation, and attendance equity—domains central to understanding the stadium as a site of economic division—yet remains largely invisible to sports business scholarship. Future work must connect vendor contracts (Aramark, Levy), labour conditions, menu governance, and the lived experience of concourse navigation to produce a fuller reading of tiered access through infrastructure.
