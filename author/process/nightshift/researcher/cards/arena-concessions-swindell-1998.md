# Arena Concessions as Franchise Revenue

Professional basketball franchises generate substantial income from concessions—a revenue stream encompassing food, beverages, and venue merchandise. Swindell (1998) provides empirical data from the mid-1990s: Texas-based teams including the Dallas Mavericks and Houston Rockets each realised approximately $2.6 million annually from concession fees (15% of total venue sales). For comparison, the Dallas Cowboys generated $6.6 million from the same mechanism.

Contemporary revenue models treat concessions as a distinct income category alongside ticket sales, premium seating, naming rights, and local broadcast deals. Gonsalves (2022) identifies premium food and beverage service—particularly in luxury seating tiers—as a strategic lever for franchise profitability. The underlying mechanism: venue operators typically capture both the concession fee (a fixed percentage of sales) plus ancillary revenue from sales taxes on food and beverages.

For new arenas, this multiplier effect compounds considerably. Swindell estimates that sales-tax revenue alone could generate $200 million in new venue financing for a Rangers stadium, or $175 million for Mavericks/Stars facilities. Consequently, concessions represent more than operational amenity; they function as a material component of franchise financial planning and public-sector subsidy justification.
