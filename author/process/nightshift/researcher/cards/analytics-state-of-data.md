**Analytics as Competitive Infrastructure**

The contemporary NBA has shifted from a handful of pioneering analytics departments (Boston Celtics, Houston Rockets) to league-wide quantitative adoption. Teams now employ dedicated analytics staff—data scientists, engineers, statisticians—processing real-time game data, player biomechanics, and contract optimisation. Major franchises operate proprietary systems (player-tracking via NBA cameras, biometric monitoring) alongside standardised metrics.

Available toolkits have expanded considerably. Beyond box-score measures (PER, win shares), teams leverage expected-value models, spatial analytics (shot location, player positioning), and off-court factors (rest cycles, travel fatigue, injury recovery). Yet standardisation remains incomplete: franchises weight VORP, BPM, EPM, and other advanced metrics differently, reflecting distinct philosophies and computational resources.

A persistent tension exists between transparency and competitive edge. The NBA publishes some data publicly (advanced stats, tracking data), but franchises guard internal models closely, creating asymmetry: well-resourced teams exploit proprietary insights whilst smaller-market franchises struggle to match analytical sophistication, potentially widening competitive inequality.
