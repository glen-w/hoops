League revenue constitutes the aggregated income streams that sustain the NBA as a commercial operation and determine distribution to individual franchises. Media rights account for the largest revenue component: domestic national contracts with ESPN and Turner Broadcasting generated approximately US$2.6 billion annually as of 2023, with additional local television deals ranging from US$9.4 million to US$149 million per franchise (Santomier et al., 2023; Neuhaus & Thomas, 2024). The nine-year national agreement signed in 2014 totalled US$24 billion, reflecting substantial growth from US$100 million in 1982–85 (Bukstein & Eisenberg, n.d.).

Ticket sales, historically significant at 29.14 % of total revenue in 2010–11, declined to 19.32 % by 2019–20 (Santomier et al., 2023). Sponsorship revenue reached US$1.46 billion in 2020–21, including the Nike apparel partnership (US$1 billion over eight years) and the jersey-patch sponsorship programme (over US$150 million incremental) (Santomier et al., 2023). NBA-branded merchandise generates over US$1 billion annually, with 45 % earned internationally. Digital platform expansion—including League Pass streaming, the Tencent partnership (US$1.5 billion exclusive digital rights in China), and the NBA 2K League—represents emerging revenue diversification. Central revenue-sharing mechanisms under Collective Bargaining Agreements distribute proceeds to franchises, affecting salary-cap calculations and long-term investment viability.

**Sources:** Santomier et al., 2023; Neuhaus & Thomas, 2024; Bukstein & Eisenberg, n.d.
