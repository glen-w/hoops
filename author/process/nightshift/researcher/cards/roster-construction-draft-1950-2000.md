# Roster Construction and the Draft, 1950–2000

The NBA draft was the primary engine of roster construction from 1950 to 2000, yet its mechanics evolved dramatically. Early territorial picks (1950–1965) let teams claim local college stars, privileging franchises in talent-rich regions. The 1966 shift to a pure reverse-order draft aimed at competitive balance, but tanking incentives emerged quickly. Lottery reform in 1985 (and weighted odds in 1990) sought to curb this, though evidence on competitive balance effects remains contested (Berri & Schmidt, 2006; Coon, 2010).

Scouting infrastructure professionalised slowly. The 1970s saw the first dedicated scouting departments (Celtics, Lakers); by the 1990s, analytics-adjacent evaluation (Dean Oliver’s early work) supplemented traditional reports. Draft position correlated strongly with career value — top-5 picks produced ~3× the Win Shares of picks 11–30 (Pelton, 2015) — yet franchise competence in development and fit mattered as much as slot.

Key gap: no synthetic history links draft mechanics, scouting evolution, and roster outcomes across the full period. Most studies isolate eras (e.g., territorial picks, lottery era) or focus on modern analytics. Primary sources — league minutes, team scouting reports, agent correspondence — remain underexploited.
