## Analytics: How to Win

**Sources:** Oliver (2004); Zhong (2025); Xiao et al. (2025); Demenius (n.d.)

The Four Factors framework, introduced by Dean Oliver (2004), identifies four statistical categories as the primary determinants of NBA team success: shooting (effective field goal percentage), turnover management, offensive rebounding, and free throw execution. This foundational model established structured statistical analysis as the basis for understanding winning teams, with subsequent research by Gómez et al. and Kubatko et al. confirming that these four factors effectively distinguish winning from losing teams across professional leagues [Xiao et al., 2025].

Contemporary analytics extends well beyond Oliver's original framework. Zhong (2025) reaffirms that the Four Factors remain "key determinants of team success," whilst Demenius (n.d.) identifies a critical scholarly gap: most basketball research relies on traditional box-score measures, yet there remains "a lack of wider studies of finishing actions frequency and efficiency" in NBA playoff competition. Recent work by Xiao et al. (2025) documents the evolution from structured technical statistics to machine-learning methods, including unsupervised clustering of player-tracking data and spatiotemporal deep-learning frameworks that enable franchises to identify defensive strategies and analyse playtype efficiency with unprecedented precision.

The analytical landscape reflects a central tension: franchises now integrate traditional Four Factors analysis with advanced metrics to inform roster construction, draft evaluation, and in-game tactics, yet the gap between analytical insight—what data reveals wins—and strategic execution remains a fundamental franchise challenge.

{Oliver, 2004}
