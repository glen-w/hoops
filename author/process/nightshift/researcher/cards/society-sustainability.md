**Society: Sustainability in professional basketball**

Professional basketball franchises operate within contexts of environmental and labour sustainability that shape long-term viability and community impact. NBA arenas consume significant energy and resources; franchise decisions on facility modernisation, renewable energy investment, and waste management reflect broader capitalist incentives toward profit over environmental stewardship. Labour sustainability—fair wages, benefits, and long-term worker support for players, coaches, and arena workers—remains contested across franchise decision-making and collective bargaining frameworks.

Scholarly analysis should address: environmental footprints of arena operations and travel (carbon, energy, waste); franchise investment in community sustainability and long-term neighbourhood development; labour sustainability frameworks (living wages, worker equity, retirement security); and how franchise ownership structures incentivise or constrain sustainability commitments. Climate change, worker precarity, and franchise longevity intersect with capitalism analysed in the prior card. A sustainability lens connects operational transparency, stakeholder accountability, and whether franchises prioritise short-term returns over durable community and environmental stewardship from 2000 to 2026.
