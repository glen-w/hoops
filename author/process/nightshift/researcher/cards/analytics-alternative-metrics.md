# Alternative Metrics in NBA Analytics

**Source key:** Grounded

Beyond basic box-score totals, NBA organisations employ advanced metrics to isolate individual and team impact. Value Over Replacement Player (VORP), as defined by Johnson & Minuci (2020), measures a player's on-court contribution relative to a replacement-level player (set at −2.0 points per 100 possessions), with the product multiplied by 2.70 yielding wins-above-replacement (Sarlis & Tjortjis, 2020). Box Plus/Minus (BPM) underpins VORP; other frameworks include Player Efficiency Rating (PER) and, leveraging modern tracking data, Estimated Plus/Minus (EPM) and RAPTOR.

These metrics have evolved alongside data availability. Teramoto et al. (2018) examined VORP as a predictor of draft success; Chauhan et al. (2021) employed it to quantify post-injury performance recovery. Gutiérrez (2024) documents strong correlations between VORP and competitive success, whilst Terner & Franks (2021) highlight the enduring value of box-score approaches for historical player evaluation predating play-by-play datasets. Modern front offices typically triangulate across multiple frameworks, acknowledging that no single metric captures the full complexity of basketball performance (Sarlis et al., 2021).
