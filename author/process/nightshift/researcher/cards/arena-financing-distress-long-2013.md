**Municipal Fiscal Risk and Arena Financing Covenant Stress**

Public financing of professional sports arenas introduces structural fiscal vulnerability to municipal budgets, particularly when revenue projections—tourism development taxes, tax-increment financing, hotel-motel levies—rest on optimistic growth assumptions. {Long, 2013} frames arena financing as a risk-transfer mechanism whereby municipalities absorb downside exposure while franchises and developers capture upside returns. The 2008 financial crisis demonstrated these fragilities acutely: post-crisis deflation in taxable sales and tourism revenues triggered covenant stress and downgrade cycles in multiple arena-backed bonds. The Amway Center case (Orlando, 2008–2010) exemplifies this pattern: bonds backed by a newly raised tourism development tax were downgraded to junk status within two years, with default warnings issued for 2012. This scenario—initial municipal enthusiasm for public financing, followed by rapid fiscal deterioration when revenue assumptions prove overly optimistic—repeats across arena deals and merits comparative analysis. Understanding how covenant failures propagate and how municipal authorities restructure these obligations illuminates the distribution of financial risk between public and private stakeholders in sports facility financing.
