Amway Center (Orlando NBA) offers a grounded case of tourism development tax (TDT) covenant distress post-2010. The City of Orlando issued bonds backed by an increase in the tourism development tax to finance the arena. Within two years of bond issuance (circa 2010), the city's bond rating was downgraded to junk status with a default warning for 2012. The immediate trigger was a shortfall in tourism tax receipts — the assumption that transient-occupancy and rental taxes would grow to service arena debt proved optimistic when actual tourism demand and tax collections lagged projections. Unlike the Little Caesars case (TIF-based, property-tax dependent), Amway illustrates TDT distress tied to economic volatility (tourism demand recession, post-recession recovery delay). The downgrade occurred during a period when arena operating costs and debt service remained locked in while revenue sources contracted. This case exemplifies the broader pattern: public authorities issued bonds assuming sustained growth in earmarked tax streams; when economic conditions diverged, franchises faced covenant pressure and public agencies risked default. The 2012 default warning suggests the city contemplated restructuring, illustrating how arena finance structures couple public creditworthiness directly to arena performance and franchise operating risk.
