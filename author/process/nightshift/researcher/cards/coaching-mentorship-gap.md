**Coaching Mentorship and Tactical Innovation: Infrastructure vs. Causation**

Development programs and mentorship structures in NBA coaching are documented across institutional histories and coaching lineage records (76TGEA3M, PG39NX7L, WV9YK2V2, Q6Q6WFAV, E5HXVDT4, SYW7FJ6L, HV82UUGJ). These sources establish that coaching schools, clinics, and professional development networks existed and were active in shaping coaching philosophy from mid-twentieth century onwards. However, the biographical evidence linking specific mentor–protégé relationships to measurable tactical shifts or strategic innovations remains fragmented. Available sources document the existence of development infrastructure but do not systematically trace how individual mentors drove specific innovations (e.g., zone defence adoption, pace-and-space offence, player development methods) into broader league practice. Resolving this causal chain requires targeted biographical case studies or primary source interviews with coaches explicitly discussing their mentors' influence on their tactical choices.
