## NBA Print Sportswriting and Sports Media, 1950–2000 (PENDING)

{Neuhaus & Thomas, 2023; Rintala & Birrel, 1984; Bryant, 1980; Hilliard, 1984; Byers & Eikenmeyer, 1994; Calia, 1993; Gamache, 2023}

Before broadcast expansion, print journalism was the NBA's dominant media channel. Neuhaus & Thomas note the league "existed without the focus of live television" and relied on fragmented local coverage. The scholarly record emphasizes gendered framing in sports journalism: Rintala & Birrel (1984) found newspapers portrayed women athletes as "attractive objects rather than skilled athletes"; Bryant (1980) documented sexist language; Hilliard (1984) found similar magazine patterns; Byers & Eikenmeyer (1994) show less than one-third of Midwestern newspaper coverage addressed women's athleticism. While these studies target women's basketball, they reveal contemporary print conventions in sports media.

The 1961 Sports Broadcast Act accelerated television's dominance. Gamache (2023) documents that ESPN's rise in the late 1980s–1990s "made national figures out of local sportswriters" and reshaped how magazines and newspapers cover live sports. Hutton (2022) examines the NBA's 1970s unpopularity, suggesting archival material on press narratives during that decline.

**Gap:** Sustained scholarly treatment of NBA-specific sportswriting, franchise-by-franchise newspaper coverage, and editorial independence of print journalists remains limited. Primary sources (newspaper archives, columnist papers) would clarify this period.
