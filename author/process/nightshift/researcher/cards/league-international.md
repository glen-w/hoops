## International Basketball Competitive Balance: Non-NBA Models

The NBA's competitive balance mechanisms—draft lottery, salary caps, revenue sharing—operate within a closed system. International basketball reveals parallel ecosystems shaped by different regulatory choices. Top European clubs broke from FIBA governance in 2000 to form the EuroLeague, a semi-closed league with long-term licences granted on economic rather than sporting criteria (Karamürsel, 2023). Unlike the NBA, EuroLeague teams compete without uniform spending restrictions, creating asymmetric competitive conditions.

Research comparing EuroLeague to NBA shows the European competition suffers lower competitive balance (Karamürsel, 2023), partly due to lack of rational economic models—many teams depend entirely on wealthy benefactors. The Chinese Basketball Association, before 2020 regulatory reform, demonstrated how championships concentrate among deep-pocketed franchises, suggesting market forces without draft constraints or caps produce persistent hierarchies. Home advantage effects operate differently across international systems, raising questions about whether parity mechanisms are structural or environmental.

For the NBA considering international expansion, these models illuminate regulatory trade-offs: closing the league to redistribute talent versus open markets producing wealth concentration. International systems provide natural experiments in how competitive balance emerges without North American regulation.
