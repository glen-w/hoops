**Broadcast marketing**

The NBA's broadcasts function simultaneously as game transmission and as coordinated marketing for players, teams, and the league. Scott et al. (2014) analyse ABC's 2007 Finals coverage, showing that deliberate commentator framing directly amplifies "advertising and sponsorship dollars"; ABC deliberately structured commentary with "polysemic structures (multiple storylines)" to increase viewership and loyalty. Falcous and Maguire (2006) identify UK-produced NBA broadcasts as shaped by "professional craft and artistic elements of television production designed to produce mimetic excitement," reinforced by "time-slot allocation and promotional levels." Ji (2023) notes that "each season's schedule is carefully choreographed and calculated to ensure maximum audience attention," coordinated with sponsorship fees approximating $100 million per season in the US. Yet the scholarly literature remains remarkably sparse on how replay selection, graphic design, colour grading, and close-up frequency prioritise commercially valuable players or franchises. Understanding broadcast-as-marketing requires deeper empirical analysis of visual design choices, replay strategy, and their measurable commercial effects.
