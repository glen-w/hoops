The NBA Players Association was formed by Bob Cousy in 1954, not 1963 as sometimes presumed, though the league did not formally recognize it until January 1957. The union emerged in an era when players lacked free-agency rights and remained bound to franchises by the reserve clause. Early labour struggles centred on wage disputes and the reserve system itself.

The Oscar Robertson Rule voided the reserve clause and established free agency, fundamentally reshaping player compensation. The Haywood v. NBA ruling (1971) further expanded rights by removing draft-entrant eligibility restrictions. In 1957, Congress held hearings on organized professional team sports where the NBA released financial data under scrutiny. The NBAPA later challenged the 1976 ABA–NBA merger on antitrust grounds, ultimately securing free-agency rights through negotiated settlement.

Secondary academic sources reference these pivotal events but do not enumerate archival collections or oral-history repositories. Primary research requires consulting Congressional archives, union papers, and institutional records directly.
