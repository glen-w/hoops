# NBA Apparel Partnership Revenue Evolution, 2000–2026

Corporate apparel and licensing partnerships represent an increasingly significant revenue stream for the NBA, though often overshadowed by broadcast rights. The league's partnership with Nike (effective 2017) is valued at US $1 billion over eight years, a 245 percent increase from the prior Adidas agreement, reflecting the strategic shift toward premium global branding. International expansion amplified this value: the 2008 Chinese partnerships generated over US $50 million, while Anta's 2014 deal (US $32.7 million) and Tencent's 2015 streaming partnership (US $500 million) demonstrate how regional markets rewarded exclusive licensing rights.

NBA-branded merchandise alone generates over US $1 billion annually, with roughly 45 percent earned internationally. Player apparel endorsements—notably the Air Jordan line—add substantial individual-franchise revenue not reflected in league aggregates. These partnerships distribute asymmetrically: marquee teams and high-profile players capture disproportionate sponsorship value, amplifying regional wealth disparity beyond what broadcast-rights formulas address.

*Sources:* Roberts (2022), Patel (2019), McFarlane (2021), Wei (2008), Daily Business Network (2015).
