# ESPN's Visual Basketball Narratives, 1979–2017

Colas (2017) examines how ESPN shaped basketball's historical imagination through broadcast visualization techniques and narrative framing. From early SportsCenter segments to modern statistical graphics, ESPN's production methods—animation, overlays, archival assembly—constructed particular ways of seeing basketball's past. This was not mere reporting but active historiography: ESPN embedded ideologies about athleticism, competition, and race within visual technologies and commentary traditions.

Relevant to the book's MEDIA section inquiry: how do journalistic and broadcast institutions narrate professional basketball to mass audiences? Colas documents the evolution of sports television production from the 1970s forward, showing how visual media and sound design shape popular understanding of basketball's history and meaning. The visualization of statistics, replays, and historical footage became a form of historical consciousness-making—a technology through which millions experienced and understood the sport.

Key takeaway: broadcast and digital media do not transparently reflect basketball; they construct interpretations. Understanding modern basketball's cultural position requires attending to how ESPN, commentary traditions, and sports journalism actively frame the game's significance.

**Source**: Colas, 2017, *Visualizing Basketball's Past: The Historical Imagination of ESPN's Basketball* (22W7MVP2)
