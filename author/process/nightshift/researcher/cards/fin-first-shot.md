# Opening Money Argument

The National Basketball Association's transformation from a modest mid-century leisure enterprise into a global sports media and entertainment conglomerate has been fundamentally shaped by capital flows, ownership structures, and revenue dynamics. Understanding this financial architecture is essential to any critical analysis of franchise decision-making, labour practices, and community engagement. From the valuation of franchises (which rose from millions to billions over five decades) to the distribution of league revenues, player salaries, and operating costs, money has been the engine driving strategic choices about stadium investment, workforce management, and media relations.

This section examines how NBA franchises generate, allocate, and defend their revenue streams; how ownership models influence franchise behaviour; and how financial constraints and incentives shape relationships with players, workers, fans, and local communities. The period from 2000 to 2026 marks a critical inflection point: media rights valuations tripled, franchise valuations soared, yet labour disputes intensified, community investment stagnated, and income inequality within basketball labour expanded. Any examination of franchise accountability must begin with how money flows, who profits, and who bears the costs.
