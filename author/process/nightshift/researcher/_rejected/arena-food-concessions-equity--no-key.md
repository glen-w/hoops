**Arena Food Service: From Commodity to Cultural Equity**

Contemporary NBA arenas have undergone a quiet shift from standardised stadium fare toward curated food experiences that gesture toward dietary diversity and accessibility. Major venues now routinely stock vegan and allergen-conscious options, partner with regional suppliers, and experiment with menu innovation—moves driven by both competitive franchise positioning and real changes in fan expectations around food quality and cultural representation. Yet this expansion masks a fundamental equity gap: premium concourse experiences in luxury seating tiers offer substantially different menus, pricing, and queue management than standard-concourse service, effectively stratifying food access alongside ticket price. The infrastructure itself—concourse bottlenecks, point-of-sale systems, queue psychology—shapes both operational margins and the lived fan experience in ways that scholarship on arena design has only begun to examine (Author, YYYY). Understanding how franchises choreograph food service across socioeconomic tiers, and how design choices either reinforce or contest attendance inequality, proves essential to reading the stadium as a site where cultural aspiration meets economic division.
