# Does fatigue degrade prefrontal inhibition and working memory, and so the control reinvestment needs?

**Request:** 2026-10-22-overtime-researcher-04 (paper, med)
**Status:** done. There's good evidence for parts of the chain, but the full chain is untested.
**Sources:** OpenAlex, Crossref, Semantic Scholar and PubMed or publisher abstracts. Every DOI below resolved. Zotero keys are in brackets.

## Bottom line (suggestions, not orders)
1. **"Central fatigue" covers two different things.** The literature splits cleanly:
   - **Exercise-induced (physical) fatigue → executive function.** Hard or exhausting exercise *does* transiently hurt some executive measures. The effect is intensity-dependent and often small:
     - Chang 2012 and McMorris & Hale 2012 (meta-analyses) report small overall effects; moderate exercise can even *speed* cognition.
     - Labelle 2013 found switching errors rose with intensity.
     - Schmit 2015 found more fast impulsive errors and weaker online correction near exhaustion, but **selective response inhibition held up**.
     - Rooks 2010 found that prefrontal oxygenation falls at very hard intensities, which is the physiological basis of "transient hypofrontality" (Dietrich 2003; Dietrich & Audiffren 2011). Those two papers are **theoretical models**, not tests.
   - **Mental fatigue / ego depletion → self-control under pressure.** This is the closer match to "reinvestment needs executive control". Englert & Bertrams 2012 found that depleted players' **free-throw** accuracy fell as anxiety rose, and that pattern didn't appear in non-depleted players. Englert et al. 2015 (darts) found depletion plus high anxiety shortened the final fixation and hurt performance. Shaabani et al. 2020 found depletion hurt free throws under pressure and that a short mindfulness session prevented it. Filipas et al. 2021 found mental fatigue and sleep restriction each modestly cut free-throw accuracy. Habay et al. 2021 conclude mental fatigue impairs sport-specific psychomotor performance.
2. **There's a twist the chapter may like.** In the reinvestment literature, fatigue mostly hurts **explicitly** learned skills, while implicit (low working-memory) skills hold up:
   - Poolton et al. 2007: explicit learners deteriorated after double Wingate tests and implicit learners didn't.
   - Masters et al. 2008: the same result after an exhaustive VO2max run.
   - Banihosseini et al. 2025 replicated it for both physical and mental fatigue.
   So the evidence fits "fatigue hurts the skill that *depends on* conscious control" better than "fatigue makes you reinvest". Those two claims make opposite predictions about which players suffer.
3. **Not directly tested anywhere found:** a single study measuring (a) fatigue, (b) a prefrontal inhibition or working-memory marker and (c) reinvestment or choking under pressure in the same athletes. The chain "fatigue → prefrontal inhibition → reinvestment failure" is an inference from separate literatures.

### Fatigue × implicit vs explicit control (the twist)
- Poolton, Masters & Maxwell (2007). Passing thoughts on the evolutionary stability of implicit motor behaviour: performance retention under physiological fatigue. *Conscious Cogn* 16(2), 456–468. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.concog.2006.06.008 [V693HCNS]. After double Wingate tests, explicit learners declined and implicit learners didn't.
- Masters, Poolton & Maxwell (2008). Stable implicit motor processes despite aerobic locomotor fatigue. *Conscious Cogn* 17(1), 335–338. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.concog.2007.03.009 [PGRIUACI]. Errorless (implicit) throwing held up after an exhaustive VO2max run, and errorful (explicit) throwing didn't.
- Banihosseini, Abdoli & Kavyani (2025). *Front Psychol* 16, 1438313. https://doi.org/10.3389/fpsyg.2025.1438313 [TSCM8R7E]. The implicit group outperformed under both physical and mental fatigue (n = 32).

## Where the evidence is thin, and a suggested reframing
- **No study links fatigue → measured prefrontal inhibition or working memory → reinvestment → free-throw outcome within the same people.**
- Ego-depletion evidence carries a replication caveat. The large multi-lab replications in social psychology were largely null. Worth a footnote.
- One alternative the data support better: *fatigue removes the working-memory resources that explicit, consciously controlled shooting relies on.* Players whose free throw is "rule-based" then suffer most, which links to researcher-03's automaticity baseline. That is a testable reframing, not an instruction.
