## Core argument
Crowd noise may influence free-throw accuracy. The pandemic seasons played without spectators and a small field experiment are used to explore the idea.

## Methods
1. **Secondary league data.** Free-throw percentages of the top-5 teams in EuroLeague (2016-17 vs 2020-21) and NBA (2017-18 vs 2019-20) seasons.
2. **Pilot experiment.** 10 junior female players at the "Rīdzene" sports school shot free throws at rest, after physical load, and under a noise condition (noise task: 5 players).

## Key findings
- **EuroLeague:** top-5 FT accuracy was 3.37% higher in 2020-21 (no fans) than in 2016-17.
- **NBA:** the best team shot 83.4% in 2019-20, against 81.5% in 2017-18.
- **Pilot:** 68.4% at rest, 70.0% after physical fatigue, and 67.6% under noise. The differences are trivial and were not tested meaningfully.

## Critical read: weak evidence
- **The NBA comparison is flawed.** Most of the 2019-20 regular season was played **in front of full crowds**: about 971 games before the 11 March 2020 suspension, with roughly 17.3M attendance per Basketball-Reference (see coder-03). Only the bubble restart had no fans, so 2019-20 can't serve as a "no-spectator" season.
- Comparing a top-5 or best team across seasons doesn't control for roster, rule or trend effects.
- The pilot's n of 5–10 and its null-looking differences don't support a causal sound effect.

## Fit with the chapter (`chapters/08_sound_free_throws.md`)
