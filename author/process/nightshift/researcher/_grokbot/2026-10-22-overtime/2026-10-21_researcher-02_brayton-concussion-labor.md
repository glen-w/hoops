## Core argument
Popular media covers concussions as an informative health story but avoids treating the athlete as a **manual worker** whose body wears out and becomes disposable under late capitalism. Working conditions can't be separated from concussion risk.

## Methods
- Critical discourse analysis (Fairclough tradition) of North American popular online press from 2011 on.
- Theoretical lens: socialist and Marxist sport sociology (Beamish, Brohm, Gruneau, Hoch, Rigauer and others).

## Key findings
Two recurring narratives push labour out of the story:
1. **"Millionaires versus billionaires."** Lockouts are framed as greed on both sides, which minimises athletes' labour grievances.
2. **Self-responsibility.** Blame shifts from league deniability to athletes' personal choices about risk.

Together these stop readers from seeing brain injury as something that arises from specific relations of production.

**Limitations:** the study covers media texts only, with no audience or reception research and no social media. The authors also acknowledge that Marxist sport sociology has been criticised as deterministic.
