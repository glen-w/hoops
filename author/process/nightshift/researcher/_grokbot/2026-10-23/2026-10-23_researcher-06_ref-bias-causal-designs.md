# Referee bias and home advantage: what the natural experiments show (2010–2026)

**Request:** 2026-10-22-researcher-06 · **Status:** done (one claim unverified) · **Prepared by:** Grok Bot nightshift, 23 Oct 2026

## Bottom line (suggestions, not orders)
- **COVID empty-arena games are the best natural experiment we have, and in basketball they mostly *don't* show referees as the channel.** NBA home advantage collapsed without fans: about +2.1 points with fans vs about +0.4–0.44 without. But studies that look directly at calls find little or no change in *biased* calls (Gong 2022; Ehrlich & Potter 2022; Szabó 2022; Ganz & Allsop 2023). The effect looks more like player effort and energy, e.g. rebounding (Leota et al. 2022).
- **Soccer is different.** There, empty stadiums clearly made referees fairer to away teams (Pettersson-Lidbom & Priks 2010; Scoppa 2021; Bryson et al. 2021). The book should keep the two sports apart.
- **European basketball is mixed.** EuroLeague home advantage fell from about 70% to about 51%, with a change in free-throw attempts (Paulauskas et al. 2022). In Spain's ACB, fouls shifted but EuroLeague showed no foul effect (Jiménez et al. 2025).
- **Before COVID, the best basketball evidence of referee favouritism is about discretionary calls.** Price, Remer & Stone (2012) find home teams, and teams trailing in a game or series, get more of the judgement-call turnovers.

## Fact check: "Allsop & Ganz 2021" and FiveThirtyEight's "1.4 points"
- The author order is **Ganz & Allsop**. The July 2021 AEI working paper "All-star fans and home court advantage" [SNHXZZQV] estimated **about 1.4 points of home advantage per 1,000 fans** (IV estimate; 1.38 without instruments). Raw figures: 2.13 points with fans vs 0.39 without.
- The FiveThirtyEight article ("After This Weird NBA Season, We Have a Better Idea of How Much Fans Matter", 6 Jul 2021) [XXJZJ7XE] was written **by Scott Ganz himself**. It is the same analysis, *not* an independent confirmation: 440 empty games averaged +0.39, 507 games with fans +2.13, and the prior six seasons +2.55. I verified it through AEI's republication; I could not confirm the fivethirtyeight.com URL.
- **The published version updates the numbers.** Ganz & Allsop, "A Mere Fan Effect on Home-Court Advantage", *J Sports Econ* 25(1):30–53 (online 2023), DOI 10.1177/15270025231200890 [3FW9V7JG], reports **2.13 vs 0.44 points and about 1.7 points per 1,000 fans** (about 2.2 extra home wins a season). → **Use 1.7 (published) rather than 1.4 (working paper / 538).**
- Both versions find that attendance does **not** significantly change the foul margin. So the fan effect in this paper does *not* run through referees.
- The "~1.3 extra free-throw attempts per game for home teams" claim: **not verified**; I couldn't find its source. Don't use it until it's sourced.

## Where evidence is thin
- No study of NBA free-throw attempts with fans vs without was verified tonight, and the "1.3 FTA" claim is unsourced.
- Crowd *noise* has been tested experimentally in soccer, and in basketball for anxious refs (2PBTRNY6, already in the library), but not causally in NBA games.

## What the book can safely say
When COVID emptied NBA arenas, home-court advantage almost vanished: from about two points a game to under half a point. The best estimate is that every 1,000 fans add roughly 1.7 points (Ganz & Allsop 2023). But when researchers looked at the referees' calls, including the league's own Last Two Minute Reports, they found little sign that NBA officials had been tilting calls toward the home team because of the crowd. The fans seem to work mainly through the players. Soccer tells a different story: there, empty stadiums clearly made referees fairer to visitors.
