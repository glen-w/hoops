# Media rights revenue history: 1990–2026

## Timeline grounded from web sources (2026-10-23)

### Cable era (1990s–2000s)
- **Pre-2002:** NBC national broadcasts; local cable arrangements
- **2002–2014:** ABC/ESPN/TNT six-year deal with AOL Time Warner
  - Shift to cable-dominant model
  - ESPN dual revenue (advertisers + subscribers) outpaced free OTA networks
  - ABC retained Finals and weekend games
  - Revenue impact: Boston Celtics 10-K projected $5M drop in local TV+cable revenue

### Cable dominance (2004–2014)
- **2004–2013:** NBA rights under earlier ESPN/TNT agreement
- **2014–present:** Nine-year ESPN/Turner deal (announced 2014)
  - **Revenue jump:** $966M/year → $2.6B/year (nearly 3× increase)
  - Established current template

### Cord-cutting pressure (2015–2024)
- **2021 snapshot:** ESPN distribution fell to 76M US homes (from 100M decade prior; −24% peak-to-trough)
- Cable-only model vulnerable; networks needed new revenue streams

### Current deal (2025–2035, pending finalization)
- **Announced framework (2024):** ~$76B over 11 years ($2.5B+/year estimated)
- **Partners:** NBC/Peacock, Disney/ESPN+, Amazon Prime Video
- **Streaming shift:** Half of NBC games exclusive to Peacock; Amazon tier ~$1.8B/year
- **Status:** Pending Warner Bros. Discovery legal challenge (TNT displacement)
