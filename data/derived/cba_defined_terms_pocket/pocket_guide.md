# CBA Defined-Terms Pocket Guide

*A vignette glossary of the NBA's collective bargaining machinery*

---

## Basketball Related Income (BRI)

**Editions:** 2017, 2023  
**Locators:** `cba:2017:art-I:sec-1:(f)` | `cba:2023:art-I:sec-1:(h)`

The league's take. Everything from ticket sales and broadcast deals to the fifty-seven varieties of NBA-licensed socks flows through this accounting vessel. Players get their negotiated share; owners get theirs. Think of BRI as the river that feeds both the salary cap and the benefit pools—only what's measured here can be divided. The formal definition lives in Article VII's revenue waterfall, but the term itself anchors nearly every financial rule in the Agreement.

**Related:** [Salary Cap](#salary-cap), [Total Salaries and Benefits](#total-salaries-and-benefits)

## Salary Cap

**Editions:** 2017, 2023  
**Locators:** `cba:2017:art-I:sec-1` | `cba:2023:art-I:sec-1`

The official ceiling—but a soft one. Every July 1, the league announces a number (tied to prior-year BRI) that no team's payroll may exceed… unless they invoke one of the dozen-plus Exceptions carved out elsewhere in the Agreement. Teams can go over the cap to re-sign their own stars, to claim a player off waivers, to use the taxpayer mid-level. The cap is less a wall than a base camp: most contending teams live in the luxury-tax atmosphere above it.

**Related:** [Exception](#exception), [Team Salary](#team-salary), [Room](#room)

## Team Salary

**Editions:** 2017, 2023  
**Locators:** `cba:2017:art-I:sec-1` | `cba:2023:art-I:sec-1`

What the cap actually measures. Sum of all player salaries on a team's books (active, inactive, waived-but-not-yet-stretched), plus deemed amounts (trade kickers, likely bonuses), minus permitted credits. Article VII's accounting rules turn a pile of contracts into one number that the league compares against the Salary Cap and the luxury-tax thresholds every day.

**Related:** [Salary Cap](#salary-cap), [Likely Bonus](#likely-bonus), [Unlikely Bonus](#unlikely-bonus)

## Exception

**Editions:** 2017, 2023  
**Locators:** `cba:2017:art-I:sec-1:(y)` | `cba:2023:art-I:sec-1:(x)`

The league's loopholes, codified. An Exception lets a team exceed the Salary Cap—whether to re-sign their own free agent (Bird Rights), to replace an injured star (Disabled Player), or to add a mid-tier veteran when already over the cap (Mid-Level). The CBA names each exception, sets its dollar limit and duration, and specifies which moves consume it. Mastery of the Exceptions separates front-office wizards from mere spreadsheet operators.

**Related:** [Salary Cap](#salary-cap), [Room](#room)

## Room

**Editions:** 2017, 2023  
**Locators:** `cba:2017:art-I:sec-1` | `cba:2023:art-I:sec-1`

Cap space, in the vernacular. Technically, the extent to which a team's current Team Salary sits below the Salary Cap, or the extent to which an Exception remains available. Front offices speak of "having room" or "needing room"—the practical difference between signing a free agent outright versus needing to invoke the Mid-Level or the Bi-Annual. Teams that renounce their free agents and dump salary chase room; contenders over the cap rely on Exceptions instead.

**Related:** [Salary Cap](#salary-cap), [Exception](#exception), [Team Salary](#team-salary)

## Moratorium Period

**Editions:** 2017, 2023  
**Locators:** `cba:2017:art-I:sec-1` | `cba:2023:art-I:sec-1`

The summer freeze. July 1 at 12:01 a.m. Eastern, free agency begins—but no contract can be signed until noon on July 6. Teams and players shake hands, agree to terms, leak deals to reporters, all while the league calculates the new Salary Cap from final BRI figures. The Moratorium lets everyone negotiate in a stable world where the cap number is known but the roster isn't yet locked. Then the floodgates open at noon on the sixth.

**Related:** [Salary Cap Year](#salary-cap-year), [Free Agent](#free-agent)

## Designated Veteran Player

**Editions:** 2017, 2023  
**Locators:** `cba:2017:art-I:sec-1:(q)` | `cba:2023:art-I:sec-1:(p)`

The supermax slot. A team can sign or extend one or two veterans to contracts that pay 30% to 35% of the cap—if those players meet award or All-NBA criteria and if the team holds their Bird Rights. This is how a small-market franchise locks in its homegrown MVP for six more seasons at a salary no other team can offer. The 2017 CBA introduced the two-player limit; the 2023 edition tightened eligibility. Front offices now choose which stars get the designation; the rest get merely large maxes.

**Related:** [Maximum Annual Salary](#maximum-annual-salary), [Qualifying Veteran Free Agent](#qualifying-veteran-free-agent)

## Qualifying Veteran Free Agent

**Editions:** 2017, 2023  
**Locators:** `cba:2017:art-I:sec-1:(yy)` | `cba:2023:art-I:sec-1`

The player who stayed. A Veteran Free Agent who played three full seasons with the same team (or changed teams only via trade, not by signing elsewhere) before hitting free agency. This status matters because it unlocks the team's Bird Rights—the ability to re-sign the player over the cap, to offer a fifth year, to make a Qualifying Offer and gain right of first refusal if the player is restricted. The three-year threshold rewards loyalty and continuity; it's the CBA's engine for letting teams keep their core.

**Related:** [Prior Team](#prior-team), [Early Qualifying Veteran Free Agent](#early-qualifying-veteran-free-agent), [Restricted Free Agent](#restricted-free-agent)

## Early Qualifying Veteran Free Agent

**Editions:** 2017, 2023  
**Locators:** `cba:2017:art-I:sec-1:(u)` | `cba:2023:art-I:sec-1:(t)`

The two-year version. Played two seasons (not three) with the Prior Team before free agency. Grants some Bird Rights—enough to re-sign the player over the cap—but not the full raise increments or five-year term a Qualifying Veteran enjoys. The distinction matters in extension talks: a front office might prefer to wait until the player qualifies fully, or the player might want to lock in security now rather than bet on staying healthy another year.

**Related:** [Qualifying Veteran Free Agent](#qualifying-veteran-free-agent), [Prior Team](#prior-team)

## Non-Qualifying Veteran Free Agent

**Editions:** 2017, 2023  
**Locators:** `cba:2017:art-I:sec-1:(rr)` | `cba:2023:art-I:sec-1`

The journeyman. A Veteran Free Agent who doesn't meet the two- or three-year continuity tests. Maybe he signed with a new team last summer as a free agent; maybe he played only one season before opting out. His Prior Team has no Bird Rights, no right of first refusal, no special ability to re-sign him over the cap. He's on the open market in the truest sense—any team with Room or an Exception can bid. The CBA's reward for staying put shows up in relief when you see what you lose by leaving.

**Related:** [Qualifying Veteran Free Agent](#qualifying-veteran-free-agent), [Unrestricted Free Agent](#unrestricted-free-agent)

## Rookie Scale Contract

**Editions:** 2017, 2023  
**Locators:** `cba:2017:art-I:sec-1` | `cba:2023:art-I:sec-1`

The draft's conveyor belt. Every First Round Pick signs a Rookie Scale Contract: four years, salary fixed by draft position, team options in years three and four, restricted free agency if the team declines the fourth year. The structure is identical across all thirty teams; the only variable is slot number. This uniformity is the price of draft order—no bidding wars, no negotiation over term, just sign here and start your career. By year four, both sides know whether an extension conversation is coming.

**Related:** [First Round Pick](#first-round-pick), [Rookie Salary Scale](#rookie-salary-scale), [Option](#option)

## Two-Way Contract

**Editions:** 2017, 2023  
**Locators:** `cba:2017:art-I:sec-1:(yyy)` | `cba:2023:art-I:sec-1`

The shuttle ticket. A player on a Two-Way Contract splits time between the NBA team and its G League affiliate, earning a smaller salary when in the G League and the Minimum Player Salary (prorated by days) when called up. Teams can carry two or three Two-Way Players outside their fifteen-man roster. The 2017 CBA introduced the category; the 2023 CBA expanded it. For players, it's a development path that keeps you in the organization; for teams, it's roster depth that doesn't count against the cap the way a standard contract does.

**Related:** [Standard NBA Contract](#standard-nba-contract), [NBAGL](#nbagl), [Active List](#active-list)

## Likely Bonus

**Editions:** 2017, 2023  
**Locators:** `cba:2017:art-I:sec-1` | `cba:2023:art-I:sec-1`

The bonus the cap expects. Incentive Compensation counts as a Likely Bonus—included in the player's Salary and the team's Team Salary from day one—if the triggering condition was met last season. (If the player averaged 15 points last year, a bonus for averaging 15 this year is "likely.") The practical effect: a likely bonus burns cap space now, even if the player never earns it. The opposite of an Unlikely Bonus, which is invisible to the cap until achieved.

**Related:** [Unlikely Bonus](#unlikely-bonus), [Incentive Compensation](#incentive-compensation), [Team Salary](#team-salary)

---

## Unlikely Bonus

**Editions:** 2017, 2023  
**Locators:** `cba:2017:art-I:sec-1` | `cba:2023:art-I:sec-1`

The bonus the cap ignores. Incentive Compensation excluded from Salary because the triggering condition wasn't met last season. A player who averaged 10 points can have an unlikely bonus for averaging 20; it won't count against the cap unless he actually does it, at which point the team's Team Salary rises mid-season and the league reconciles at year-end. Front offices use unlikely bonuses to structure deals that look bigger than the cap space they consume—bet on the come, pay if the bet hits.

**Related:** [Likely Bonus](#likely-bonus), [Incentive Compensation](#incentive-compensation), [Performance Bonus](#performance-bonus)

## Minimum Team Salary

**Editions:** 2017, 2023  
**Locators:** `cba:2017:art-I:sec-1` | `cba:2023:art-I:sec-1`

The payroll floor. Ninety percent of the Salary Cap (or eighty-five percent in the 2023 CBA's transitional language). Teams must spend this much on player salaries each season or distribute the shortfall to the players already on the roster. The rule prevents tanking by wallet—you can lose on purpose, but you still have to pay market rates for the players you put on the floor. Front offices that rebuild by signing veteran minimums still have to hit the floor; they just deliver the surplus as a January bonus pool.

**Related:** [Salary Cap](#salary-cap), [Minimum Player Salary](#minimum-player-salary)

## Minimum Player Salary

**Editions:** 2017, 2023  
**Locators:** `cba:2017:art-I:sec-1` | `cba:2023:art-I:sec-1`

The league's wage floor. Every Standard NBA Contract that covers a full season must pay at least the Minimum Annual Salary for the player's Years of Service. A ten-year veteran earns roughly twice what a rookie does, even at the minimum. Teams over the cap can always sign a player to the minimum without using an Exception; the league reimburses part of the veteran minimum so a team isn't penalized for signing experience. The minimum is how you fill the back end of the roster when you're out of Room.

**Related:** [Minimum Annual Salary](#minimum-annual-salary), [Years of Service](#years-of-service), [Standard NBA Contract](#standard-nba-contract)

## Maximum Annual Salary

**Editions:** 2017, 2023  
**Locators:** `cba:2017:art-I:sec-1` | `cba:2023:art-I:sec-1`

The star's ceiling. The most a player can earn in the first year of a new contract: 25%, 30%, or 35% of the Salary Cap, depending on Years of Service and (for the 35% tier) award criteria. A second-year player tops out at 25%; a seven-year vet at 30%; a ten-year All-NBA player at 35%. The max is why stars' salaries cluster—LeBron, Giannis, Embiid all earn within a few million of each other, not because they're equally valuable but because the CBA forbids paying one more. Teams compete with years, location, and winning, not dollars.

**Related:** [Designated Veteran Player](#designated-veteran-player), [Years of Service](#years-of-service), [Salary Cap](#salary-cap)

## Years of Service

**Editions:** 2017, 2023  
**Locators:** `cba:2017:art-I:sec-1` | `cba:2023:art-I:sec-1`

The career odometer. Count one Year of Service for each season the player appeared on an NBA Active or Inactive List for at least one day during the Regular Season. Years of Service determine the Minimum Player Salary, the Maximum Annual Salary tier, and (in older CBAs) the vesting schedule for pensions. The CBA tracks this automatically; you can't fake it, can't buy it, can't trade it. All you can do is show up, stay in the league, and let the years accumulate.

**Related:** [Minimum Player Salary](#minimum-player-salary), [Maximum Annual Salary](#maximum-annual-salary), [Veteran](#veteran)

## Active List / Inactive List

**Editions:** 2017, 2023  
**Locators:** `cba:2017:art-I:sec-1:(a)` | `cba:2017:art-I:sec-1` | `cba:2023:art-I:sec-1:(c)` | `cba:2023:art-I:sec-1`

The daily roster split. The Active List holds players eligible to play tonight; the Inactive List holds those who can't (injury, suspension, coach's decision). Contracts count against Team Salary whether the player is active or inactive, but only active players can suit up. Teams must submit their active roster before each game. The distinction also matters for Years of Service: you earn credit by being on *either* list during the Regular Season, not by actually playing.

**Related:** [Years of Service](#years-of-service), [Team Salary](#team-salary), [Two-Way List](#two-way-list)

## Option / Option Year

**Editions:** 2017, 2023  
**Locators:** `cba:2017:art-I:sec-1:(ss)` | `cba:2023:art-I:sec-1`

The contract's conditional year. An Option in a Player Contract gives either the team or the player the right to extend the term by one year (the Option Year) under pre-agreed terms. Rookie Scale Contracts include team options in years three and four. Veterans occasionally negotiate player options (letting them choose free agency or stay one more year) or team options (letting the franchise decide). The option's holder has leverage; the other side has certainty only until the deadline.

**Related:** [Rookie Scale Contract](#rookie-scale-contract), [Free Agent](#free-agent), [Extension](#extension)

## Prior Team

**Editions:** 2017, 2023  
**Locators:** `cba:2017:art-I:sec-1:(ww)` | `cba:2023:art-I:sec-1`

Where you were before you weren't. The team for which a player last played before becoming a Qualifying Veteran Free Agent, Early Qualifying Veteran Free Agent, or Non-Qualifying Veteran Free Agent. The Prior Team holds the player's Bird Rights (if any), can make a Qualifying Offer (if the player is restricted), and enjoys re-signing advantages baked into the CBA. The term's simplicity hides its importance: your Prior Team is the only team that *doesn't* have to compete on a level playing field.

**Related:** [Qualifying Veteran Free Agent](#qualifying-veteran-free-agent), [Restricted Free Agent](#restricted-free-agent)
