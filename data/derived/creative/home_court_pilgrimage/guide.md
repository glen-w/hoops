## Orientation: The sacred and the statistical

In the 2024 NBA playoffs, home teams won **48** of **82** games—about **58.5%**. This is not a vanishing. This is a *residual tug*, measurable in box scores but felt in bodies. The arena as pilgrimage stop. The court as ritual site where home advantage persists even when conventional wisdom declares it dead.

What follows is not a conventional playoff recap. It is a **field guide** to the 2024 postseason as a **ritual geography**: fifteen series, thirty arenas (or rather, the home venues of the thirty clubs who played), and the stubborn fact that place still mattered.

---

## I. The pilgrimage circuit: 2024 playoff series as sacred itinerary

The 2024 playoffs comprised **fifteen series** spanning **82 games**. Each series traced a path between two arenas. Each game a station. Each outcome a testament to or against the sanctity of home court.

The numbers suggest a **soft but persistent advantage**. Compare:

- **Playoffs 2024:** 58.5% home wins (48–34)
- **Regular season 2023–24:** 54.3% home wins (668–562), called "the worst home-court mark in league history" by wire services

The playoff advantage *rose* relative to the regular season. The ritual intensifies in the postseason. The home crowd's density increases. The arena becomes a **pressure vessel**.

### Landmark: The first-round Game 1 sweep

In the **first round**, home teams went **8–0** in Game 1. Five times in the modern sixteen-team era has this occurred. The opening game of a series functions as **threshold rite**: the home team asserts dominance, the visitor must prove they belong. This is not statistical noise. This is the liturgy of the playoffs.

## II. Arenas as pilgrimage stops: Typology

Not all arenas are equal. Some are **destinations** (e.g., Madison Square Garden, TD Garden). Others are **waypoints**. The 2024 playoffs did not produce a venue-by-venue win ledger—Basketball-Reference does not break out home records by arena in its series tables—but we can infer the **ritual function** of home court from the aggregate:

### Type A: The fortress (majority home series)

Where the home team wins most or all home games in a series. The arena functions as **sanctuary**. Visitors enter as supplicants. The home team's wins at home are *expected*; losses are *violations*.

### Type B: The contested ground (split series)

Where home and away alternate. The series becomes a **pilgrimage route** with no clear sanctuary. Each arena is a stop on a longer journey. Neither team can claim permanent advantage.

### Type C: The overturned shrine (road-dominated series)

Where the visitor wins more games at the opponent's arena than expected. These are the exceptions that prove the rule. They become **legend** precisely because they violate the ritual order.

The 2024 playoffs, at 58.5% home wins, suggest more Type A and Type B than Type C. The sanctity of home court was **soft but not abolished**.

---

## III. Ritual time: The game as ceremony

Each home game in the playoffs is not merely a contest but a **ceremony** with:

- **Procession:** The home team enters last. The visiting team already on the floor. The crowd standing.
- **Invocation:** National anthem. Sometimes a local singer, sometimes a celebrity. The crowd's volume calibrated to the stakes.
- **Consecration:** Tip-off. The ball in the air. The moment when home court either materializes as advantage or fails to.
- **Communion:** Timeouts. Huddles. The crowd's rhythm matching the game's.
- **Benediction or lament:** The final buzzer. Home wins ratified. Home losses mourned.

The **48 home wins** in 2024 represent 48 successful ceremonies. The **34 home losses** represent 34 failures—but not failures of the *form*, only of the *outcome*.

---

## IV. The anti-pilgrimage: Regular season as desacralized space

The 2023–24 regular season produced the lowest home-court advantage on record: **54.3%** (668–562). Why?

**Hypothesis:** The regular season is **profane time**. The schedule is relentless. The crowd is sometimes sparse. The stakes are often low until the final weeks. The arena cannot function as sanctuary when the ritual is diluted by repetition and fatigue.

The playoffs **re-sacralize** the space. Fewer games. Higher stakes. The crowd becomes a congregation again. The 58.5% home win rate in 2024 is the statistical signature of this shift.

## V. Methodological note: What we counted, what we couldn't

Our pilgrimage ledger draws exclusively from **Basketball-Reference's 2024 playoff schedule** (accessed 2026-09-19). We counted:

- **Home team definition:** The team listed after the `@` in game lines.
- **Winner:** The team with the higher final score.
- **Series count:** 15 series (Finals through first round).
- **Game count:** 82 total.

We did **not** have access to:

- Venue-by-venue home records (only series-level data)
- Attendance figures
- Crowd noise measurements
- In-game home/away splits by quarter

We leave blank what we do not know. The ritual geography is **partial**, traced from box scores and game locations. The full cartography of home court requires ethnographic attendance—beyond the scope of this guide.

---

## VI. Conclusion: The pilgrimage endures

The 2024 playoffs did not witness the "evaporation" of home-court advantage. They witnessed its **persistence** in softened form. **48–34** is a majority. It is not overwhelming, but it is not random.

The arenas remain pilgrimage stops. The home team still benefits from the ritual geography. The numbers confirm what the crowds already know: place matters, even in an age of neutral-site speculation and load management cynicism.

The **fifteen series** of 2024 traced a map. This guide is an invitation to read it.
