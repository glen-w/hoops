**Access date:** 2026-09-20  
**Companion artifacts:** `facts_used.csv` | `method.md`

## Preface: Three streams, not one

Before we speak of "the TV deal," we must name the streams:

1. **Media rights fees:** Networks/streamers → NBA (contract price for game windows)
2. **Ad sales / bookings:** Advertisers → networks/streamers (monetization of airtime)
3. **Sponsorship rights fees:** Brands → league/teams (patches, naming, entitlements)

The air is not owned by one party. It is **rented, subdivided, and monetized** by three. What follows are field notes on the first two streams—national TV and ads money—as a **media ecology** where dollars flow in different directions and confusion is profitable.

---

## I. The rights cycle: Who pays whom, and when

### Cycle 1: 2016–17 through 2024–25 (nine years)

**Partners:** ABC/ESPN (Disney) and TNT/Turner  
**Contract total:** ~$24 billion (SECONDARY: Sports Illustrated 2014)  
**Annual average framing:** "more than $2.6 billion" (SECONDARY: SI)  
**Official disclosure:** Partners and term (OFFICIAL: NBA Communications 2014-10-06); no package dollars in the release  

**Per-team national TV payout estimate:**  
- **2024–25:** ~$103 million per team (SECONDARY: Sportico)

**Confidence:** HIGH for partners/term (official NBA release); MEDIUM for contract total and per-team payout (secondary journalism, not audited).

### Cycle 2: 2025–26 through 2035–36 (eleven years)

**Partners:** ABC/ESPN (Disney), NBC/Peacock (NBCUniversal), Prime Video (Amazon)  
**Contract total:** ~$76–77 billion (SECONDARY: Sportico/CNBC)  
- Note: Competing tallies exist; we keep both (~$76B Sportico, ~$77B CNBC) and do not average
- May include concurrent WNBA in some secondary framings (not confirmed)

**Annual average framing:** ~$6.9 billion/year (derived from ~$76B / 11)  
**Partner splits (SECONDARY CNBC "people familiar"):**  
- Disney (ABC/ESPN): ~$2.62 billion/year
- NBCUniversal (NBC/Peacock): ~$2.45 billion/year
- Amazon (Prime Video): ~$1.8 billion/year

**Official disclosure:** Partners and term (OFFICIAL: NBA.com 2024-07-24); no package dollars in the release  

**Per-team national TV payout estimate:**  
- **2025–26:** ~$143 million per team (SECONDARY: Sportico; league declined to comment)

**Confidence:** HIGH for partners/term (official); MEDIUM for contract totals, partner splits, and per-team payouts (secondary, not audited).

## II. The jump: 2.9× in per-team payout

From **~$103M** (2024–25) to **~$143M** (2025–26) per team.  
Percentage increase: **~39%** in one year.

This is not merely inflation. This is a **market revaluation** of live sports rights in a streaming era where:
- Linear TV audiences fragment
- Live sports remain **appointment viewing**
- Streamers (Amazon, Peacock) bid against cable incumbents for access

The air becomes more valuable **as a scarcity**. National TV rights are one of the few inventory types that cannot be replicated or pirated in real time.

---

## III. The advertiser stream: Who pays the networks?

The rights fees (above) flow **NBA ← networks/streamers**. But advertisers pay **networks/streamers**, not the NBA directly. This is a **parallel economy**.

### National ad sales estimates (2025–26)

| Window | Estimate | Vendor/Source | Confidence |
|--------|---------|---------------|------------|
| **Full season** | ~$2.1 billion | Guideline data via Sportico | MEDIUM |
| **Regular season only** | ~$760 million | iSpot.tv via MediaPost | MEDIUM |

**Note:** These are **different windows** (full season vs regular season). Do not reconcile them by arithmetic—they come from different measurement vendors and may use different definitions of "national" or "video ad revenue."

**What this means:**  
- Advertisers spend **billions** to reach NBA audiences via network airtime
- This money does **not** appear in the ~$76–77B rights-fee contract
- Networks/streamers monetize the games **twice**: once via rights exclusivity (league payout), once via ad inventory (advertiser payout)

## IV. The sponsored moment: When the air has a title

Beyond 30-second spots, the league and networks sell **entitlements**: named moments with brand sponsors.

Examples (OFFICIAL existence; amounts UNCONFIRMED):
- **American Express:** Tip-off and halftime "moments"
- **Kia:** Awards (MVP, etc.)
- **Emirates:** NBA Cup and jersey patches

These are **sponsorship rights fees** (brands → league), not ad sales. They occupy a third stream—overlapping with broadcast but also extending to in-arena, digital, and hospitality.

We do **not** have a public rate card for these entitlements. Trade press (IEG, SponsorUnited) publishes snapshots, but fee schedules are private. The air is owned in **layers**, not a single title.

---

## V. The inventory problem: How much air is for sale?

We know:
- Networks/streamers paid ~$76–77B for eleven years of windows
- Advertisers will spend ~$2.1B in the first season (per Sportico/Guideline)
- Some in-game "moments" are separately sponsored by brands

We do **not** know:
- How many **sellable units** (30-second spots, overlays, sponsored graphics) exist per game
- How much inventory goes **unsold** or is filled with promos
- Network **makegoods** (free spots to compensate for underdelivered audiences)
- Exact rate cards (CPM, package deals, exclusivity premiums)

This is intentional opacity. The air is a **market**, and markets depend on information asymmetry. Buyers and sellers negotiate in private. Public facts are **fragments**.

---

## VI. The augmented layer: Ads that aren't commercials

Emerging inventory type (2026 context):

**Augmented / AI in-broadcast ads:**  
- **Genius Sports** + local RSN platforms: data-triggered overlays, virtual signage
- Not traditional 30-second spots but **in-game visual augmentation**
- Existence confirmed (press releases); inventory prices unknown

This is a **fourth stream** (or a hybrid of streams 2 and 3):
- Monetizes the broadcast image (like ad sales)
- But placed via league/team partnership deals (like sponsorship)
- Sold per impression or per game, not per 30-second unit

The air is not just **rented** (rights fees) or **interrupted** (commercials) but **layered** (augmented reality). The ecology thickens.

---

## VII. Local vs national: The air is not uniform

Everything above concerns **national TV** (ABC/ESPN, NBC, Amazon, TNT/Turner). But teams also sell **local media rights**:

- Regional Sports Networks (RSNs)
- Local streaming substitutes (post-Diamond/Bally bankruptcy)
- Occasional team equity stakes in distributors

**Local revenue estimate (leaguewide):** ~10% of 2024-25 revenue per Sportico reporting—but **no unified ledger** of team-by-team RSN fees exists publicly.

**Gap:** We cannot map the full air—only the national layer. The local layer is a **patchwork**, team-specific, mostly private.

---

## VIII. The prose trap: "The TV deal is a $77 billion ad market"

This is **wrong** but common. Why it persists:

1. **Rights fees** (~$76–77B) are large and quoted often
2. **Ad sales** (~$2.1B/year) are large and quoted often
3. Both involve "TV" and "money"
4. Casual writers **sum** them or blur the distinction

**Correct phrasing:**
- "The NBA's national media rights contracts total ~$76–77 billion over eleven years."
- "National ad sales in the first season are estimated at ~$2.1 billion (Guideline via Sportico)."
- Do **not** say: "The TV deal generates ~$78–79 billion."

Why? Because **advertisers pay networks**, not the league. The league gets the **rights fee** (~$6.9B/year framing). The networks keep the **ad revenue** (and must cover rights fees, production costs, talent salaries, and profit margin from it).

The air is owned in **stages**, not a single transaction.

---

## IX. The unknowable: What we leave blank

The public facts are **fragments of a ledger** we cannot complete:

- **Network P&L:** Do ABC/ESPN/NBC/Amazon make a profit on NBA rights after ad sales and costs? (Private)
- **Inventory architecture:** Sellable units per game, unsold rates, makegood frequency (Private)
- **Leaguewide RSN rollup:** Team-by-team local media fees (Anecdotal, not systematic)
- **Activation spend:** How much do sponsors spend *executing* their deals vs the rights fee itself? (Private)
- **BRI timing:** When do rights-fee dollars hit Basketball Related Income for cap calculations? (NBA-NBPA private)

We mark these as **GAP** rather than invent. The ecology is **opaque by design**.

## XI. Conclusion: The air is plural

National TV rights are not a **single deal** but an **ecology**:
- Rights fees (league revenue)
- Ad sales (network revenue)
- Sponsorship entitlements (league/team revenue)
- Local RSN fees (team-specific revenue)
- Augmented inventory (emerging hybrid)

Each stream has **different payers and payees**. The public facts are **fragments**. The private facts are **commercial secrets**.

To own the air is to own **windows**, not sky. The NBA rents game rights. Networks rent attention. Advertisers rent seconds. Sponsors rent names. The court is on the floor, but the money is **layered above it**, invisible except in press releases and quarterly earnings we cannot fully parse.

**These field notes end here.** The full ecology would require:
- Network/streamer financial filings (incomplete for partnerships)
- League-audited BRI breakdowns (private to NBA-NBPA)
- Advertiser campaign spend (vendor estimates, not invoices)
- Team-by-team RSN ledgers (mostly unknown)

We map what we can cite. The rest is **air**.
